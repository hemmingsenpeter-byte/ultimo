# Editorial and evidence assurance

Curriculum framework coverage and authored content are separate states. Framework "defined" means an authoring blueprint exists, not proof of expertise or course efficacy. Content moves through planned -> draft -> source_checked -> subject_reviewed -> pilot_tested -> approved -> published. Keep course and lesson states honest.

1. Author maps objectives and all claims to appropriate sources. Candidate books are not verified clinical or academic claims.
2. Independent reviewer checks factual accuracy, context, bias, source quality and intellectual property. Prefer reviews, textbooks, primary studies and official sources; cite exact sections.
3. Domain reviewer evaluates safety, local rules, referral boundaries, assessment validity and meaningful alternative evidence.
4. Pilot learner tests comprehension, workload and task feasibility; record feedback without private details.
5. Approver checks all gates and documents date, version, conflicts of interest and outstanding limitations.

Safety-sensitive topics include exercise, water and outdoor safety, medical and mental health, sexuality and safeguarding, law, finance, emergency response, cybersecurity and tools. Use fictional cases and qualified supervision; never publish unsupported prescriptions or diagnose learners. For current NZ rules, use current official sources at authoring time. Do not assume a law or health recommendation is globally applicable. Review dated claims periodically. AI review is useful but does not replace qualified review. All initial v0.2 reading selections remain candidates except specifically verified design references; chapter/claim verification is outstanding.
