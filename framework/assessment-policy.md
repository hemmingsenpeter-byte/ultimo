# Assessment and progression policy

## Distinct types of evidence
Baseline diagnostic (not a grade); retrieval and comprehension; guided application; independent novel application; delayed retest; portfolio/mentor observation. The learner may choose fictional cases or equivalent accessible evidence. Keep learner data outside this public repo.

## Competency levels
0: evidence not demonstrated (not a judgment of worth); 1: accurate explanation; 2: guided application; 3: independent ordinary-context application; 4: adaptive performance in unfamiliar contexts; 5: sustained expertise and appropriate mentorship; 6: cross-domain integration. Each syllabus maps four lesson competencies to a capstone artifact, and the registry gives a seven-level rubric for each. These rubrics are first-pass templates anchored to each topic; subject-specific performance thresholds and anchor examples must be refined during course authoring.

## Decision rule
For a Level 2 course pass, all four mapped outcomes must be demonstrated at Level 2 or above, using knowledge and performance evidence. Missing evidence is "not yet demonstrated", not a fail of character. No average overrides a missing or safety-critical criterion. Level 3 requires a new task without prompts, delayed reassessment and documented reviewer judgment. Levels 4–6 require multiple contexts, repeated evidence and appropriate human expertise; no automatic award from reading, AI conversations, self-rating or time logged. Avoid using metrics of wealth, health status, subjective happiness, relationship status or disability as grades.

## Evidence record (future private implementation)
Competency ID, rubric version, attempted level, evidence description or secure pointer, context, reviewer role, criteria decisions, date, feedback, accommodations, reassessment due. Separate self-reported and observed evidence. No medical or intimate details required. Offer feedback, remediation, alternative demonstration and appeal to an independent reviewer.

## Graduation
Level 3 across the agreed core competencies is the design goal, not a current accredited qualification. For regulated or safety-sensitive areas, assess literacy and appropriate referral; practical qualification requires relevant recognised external certification. The five-year stages include progressively broader capstones; validate prerequisites and scope before assigning a project.
