## Core teaching

A counterexample is an instance that conflicts with a general claim or exposes an invalid inference. The strength of the challenge depends on the claim's logical scope. One genuine exception refutes an unrestricted universal assertion; it does not necessarily refute a claim about most cases. A statistical claim requires appropriate aggregate evidence. For a deductive argument, a countermodel makes the premises true and the conclusion false. For an empirical mechanism, a contrary observation prompts examination of measurement, scope and competing explanations. Avoid moving the goalposts: if a claim changes after a challenge, acknowledge the revision rather than pretending it was always qualified. Also avoid cherry-picking a rare exception to dismiss a well-supported tendency. Good criticism improves the precision of a claim.

## Framework

Use scope–test–revise. Identify whether the claim is universal, probabilistic, causal or conditional. Construct an in-scope challenge. Verify that its facts meet the claim's conditions. Revise the claim to match evidence; specify what further observations would test the new version.

## Worked example

A fictional company says every request submitted online receives a same-day reply. A log shows one valid online request at 10 a.m. with a reply the next morning. If the claim has no exceptions, this refutes it. If the claim was '95% of valid requests receive a same-day reply', the individual exception is consistent with the claim. Check the full distribution and definitions of valid request and business day.

### Contrasting case

A hypothetical request submitted by post cannot refute a claim specifically about online requests. The example falls outside the stated population.

## Guided practice

Challenge the statement 'All popular programmes are effective.' Construct a popular programme with no improvement on its stated outcome. Explain why popularity alone cannot logically guarantee effectiveness. Then rewrite as a testable statistical claim and specify the data needed to evaluate it.

Attempt before reading the model; compare your reasoning and correct a specific gap. Record assumptions and ask what would change your conclusion.

## Independent transfer

Take three fictional claims with different logical scopes. Supply an appropriate challenge to each and document which claim changes and which remains unresolved.

## Knowledge check

2. Does one exception refute a 90% frequency claim?
