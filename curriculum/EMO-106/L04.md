# EMO-106 / L04 — Safety and follow-through

**Draft — independent academic and qualified subject review pending.** Outcome: Evaluate and justify decisions about safety and follow-through in the context of boundaries and assertiveness.

## Orientation and retrieval

Retrieve the boundary and refusal frameworks. A fictional person sets an agreed limit, but it is repeatedly ignored. What follow-through is within their authority, and when would ordinary communication no longer be an appropriate response?

## Core teaching

Follow-through concerns what happens after a limit, request or agreement is communicated. It may involve maintaining the stated availability, renegotiating an arrangement, using a formal process, ending voluntary participation when feasible, or seeking support. It is not synonymous with punishment or forcing agreement. A proposed consequence should be lawful, proportionate, feasible, within the person's control and consistent with existing duties. If circumstances change, revise the plan openly rather than making threats that cannot or should not be carried out.

Different situations require different routes. In a safe ordinary disagreement, clarification and a revised agreement may resolve the problem. Repeated harmful behaviour, harassment, intimidation or significant power imbalance may require organisational, specialist or legal advice. In family violence or coercive circumstances, direct confrontation, announcing intentions or imposing consequences may create danger. Do not assume a communication script can control the behaviour of someone causing harm. The person experiencing harm is not responsible for preventing it through perfect assertiveness. A specialist service can help consider individual options and safety planning.

In New Zealand, official Ministry of Justice victim information and Police guidance direct people in immediate danger to 111 and provide access to family-violence specialist services. These are local routes; other countries require local emergency numbers and support services. Verify contact details before relying on them. An AI tutor, peer or lesson cannot assess an individual's danger or design a personalised safety plan. Avoid collecting sensitive information, recommending secret monitoring or providing tactical instructions. In non-emergency situations, appropriate professionals or designated organisational channels can help determine a safe approach.

## Framework

**Safety–authority–options–review decision map**

First consider immediate danger: if present, seek emergency help. If threats, coercion or risk are present without an immediate emergency, consider confidential specialist support and appropriate local advice rather than prescribing confrontation. For ordinary safe disagreements, check the agreement and authority, communicate a proportionate next step, and review results. Do not exceed role, breach duties or require another person's compliance. Identify a fallback when the first option is unavailable.

This is a referral-literacy map, not a risk assessment or individual safety plan.

## Worked example

A fictional project collaborator repeatedly sends routine requests outside the agreed availability window. In a safe peer relationship, the recipient can confirm the agreed window, respond during it, and ask the coordinator to clarify who handles genuine urgent needs. If the conduct becomes harassment or threatens the person's employment, a trusted representative or appropriate formal process may be preferable. In a separate fictional domestic situation involving credible threats, the ordinary availability script is not transferable. A specialist family-violence service can help consider options; immediate danger requires local emergency assistance. The learner does not design an exit strategy or tell the person to confront the threatening individual.

### Contrasting case

A threatened consequence such as withholding someone else's essential medication or access to necessary resources is not appropriate boundary enforcement. It could cause harm and exceeds an ordinary personal limit. The correct response is to seek appropriate support and respect safety obligations.

## Guided practice

**Evaluate three fictional follow-through decisions.** A: a safe peer repeatedly requests optional assistance after a refusal. B: a manager pressures a worker to ignore a safety requirement. C: someone reports credible threats from a partner. Identify a proportionate next route, an action to avoid, and a review condition.

**Worked solution.** A: maintain the refusal and clarify availability if useful; avoid insults or retaliatory demands. B: preserve the safety requirement and use an authorised safety or organisational route; do not bypass it. C: encourage confidential specialist support if safe, and emergency help for immediate danger; do not prescribe confrontation or a personal safety plan. The exercise assesses selection of support routes, not the person's ability to control harm.

Attempt before checking the model. Identify one specific correction and apply it to an equivalent fictional case. Assess reasoning, evidence, safe boundaries and task quality rather than personal characteristics or emotional states.

## Independent transfer

Complete the boundary scripts capstone with safe ordinary requests, refusals and follow-through, plus a contrasting case requiring organisational or specialist support. Distinguish emergency assistance from routine support and explain why the plan cannot determine individual safety.
