## Core teaching

Rehearsal is an opportunity to practise components of a task before a consequential performance. A useful rehearsal is defined by its learning objective, not by how stressful it feels. It can isolate one skill, such as explaining a recommendation, then combine components after feedback. Simulation represents selected features of a real task; fidelity means the degree to which relevant features are represented. More realism is not always necessary. A paper scenario may be sufficient to practise reasoning, whereas some practical skills require qualified supervision and appropriate equipment.

Simulation education research, particularly in medical training, identifies the importance of feedback, deliberate practice, outcome measures and transfer. This does not prove that a particular presentation exercise produces the same effects. A rehearsal should align with the desired competency: if the task is explaining a decision, memorising every sentence may not test the ability to adapt to a new question. Provide an equivalent written or recorded option where suitable; a learner may decline an audience or recording. Do not deliberately increase distress, use ridicule, or insist on disclosure of personal fears.

Gradual task variation here means changing ordinary task features, such as a new fictional question or a different example, once the learner understands the basic task. It is not clinical exposure therapy. A rehearsal can include a consented observer, a clear stop rule and constructive feedback. Evaluate the quality of the explanation, accuracy and response to the question, not the person's apparent confidence or lack of nervousness. Improvement in simulation does not certify readiness for safety-sensitive or regulated work.

## Framework

**Skill–scenario–attempt–feedback–variation**

Define a specific skill and criterion. Create a low-stakes fictional task with relevant features and an accessible format. Attempt it voluntarily, with an option to stop. Receive feedback on the task and process. Correct one gap and repeat using an equivalent new case. Only vary difficulty when useful and agreed; avoid inducing distress. Document what the rehearsal cannot establish.

## Worked example

A fictional learner prepares a two-minute explanation comparing two community venues. In round one, they use notes to describe cost and accessibility. Feedback identifies that they omitted the source of a capacity figure. In round two, they explain a different pair of venues, stating source and uncertainty. In round three, an optional fictional question asks how their decision changes if availability changes. The observer assesses accuracy, rationale and handling of uncertainty. There is no penalty for using notes, requesting clarification or choosing a written response.

### Contrasting case

A mock interview that deliberately insults participants or threatens their livelihood is not a suitable educational rehearsal. Simulated humiliation is not required to practise answering a difficult question. Clinical treatment of anxiety is outside this course's scope.

## Guided practice

**Design two rehearsal rounds.** A fictional learner must explain why a committee should compare total costs rather than headline prices. Specify a criterion, two equivalent cases and one observer question. Include a stop/opt-out rule.

**Worked solution.** Criterion: identify the relevant cost components and one uncertainty. Case A compares room hire plus cleaning; case B compares equipment rental plus delivery. Question: 'What changes if the additional fee is uncertain?' Model answer: state the known totals, flag the missing fee and compare plausible bounds rather than inventing a figure. Give feedback on the explanation, then use a different case. Written rehearsal is equally acceptable.

Attempt before consulting the worked solution. Correct one specific reasoning gap and retry with an equivalent fictional case. Assess reasoning and safe boundaries, not the learner's own health or circumstances.

## Independent transfer

Create a three-round voluntary rehearsal plan for the capstone presentation. Define one objective per round, concrete feedback criteria and safe access alternatives. Include a novel question and a condition for stopping or seeking appropriate support.
