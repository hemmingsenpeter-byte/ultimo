# MEN-107 / L03 — Process and outcomes

**Draft — independent academic and qualified subject review pending.** Outcome: Apply process and outcomes in the context of performance under pressure.

## Orientation and retrieval

Recall the difference between a learning criterion and a feeling of confidence. A fictional presenter delivers an accurate recommendation but the committee rejects it. What evidence would help evaluate the quality of the performance?

## Core teaching

An outcome is the result of an activity, such as a committee decision, a completed task or an audience response. A process criterion concerns the actions and reasoning used to produce the result. The two are related but not identical. A careful recommendation may be rejected because of competing priorities; an inaccurate recommendation may be accepted because the audience does not notice an error. Evaluation should use both the quality of reasoning and the observed result while acknowledging factors outside the performer’s control.

Outcome bias research, covered in INT-105, describes how knowledge of an outcome can influence evaluation of the preceding decision. Applying this distinction to practice does not mean ignoring outcomes. They can reveal shortcomings in assumptions or communication. It means judging the process against information and requirements available at the time, then examining the outcome as additional evidence. A useful process goal is within the learner's influence: state the recommendation, cite evidence, acknowledge uncertainty or ask a clarifying question. A desired audience decision is not wholly controllable.

Performance measures can also be imperfect proxies. Timing may matter when a deadline is real, but speaking rapidly is not inherently better than communicating clearly. Visible composure is not a reliable measure of understanding or worth. A rubric should prioritise task relevance, accuracy, rationale, accessibility and ethical conduct. Safety-critical requirements cannot be averaged away by a strong score elsewhere. The learner can use feedback to revise one controllable action without interpreting rejection as personal failure.

## Framework

**Result–process–context–next action scorecard**

Before rehearsal, write the intended result and three observable process criteria. Afterward, record the observed outcome and evidence for each criterion. Identify factors outside the performer’s control and uncertainty. Select one specific process change. Keep the result and process ratings separate; no rating of character, mental health or confidence is required.

## Worked example

A fictional presenter recommends venue B because it meets access needs at a lower total cost. The committee selects venue A for a previously undisclosed scheduling reason. The presenter correctly stated the evidence and uncertainty and answered questions honestly, but did not ask whether the schedule was a hard constraint. The rejected recommendation is not proof of poor presentation. The process review identifies a concrete improvement: confirm non-negotiable scheduling requirements earlier. The outcome supplies new information about the decision, while the existing cost calculation remains valid under its assumptions.

### Contrasting case

A presenter obtains agreement by inventing a statistic. The favourable outcome cannot justify the inaccurate process. Ethical and factual criteria remain essential even when the immediate result appears successful.

## Guided practice

**Evaluate a synthetic rehearsal.** Criteria: state conclusion, show comparable evidence, acknowledge one uncertainty, answer the question. Learner A meets all four but the simulated audience disagrees. Learner B gains agreement but omits evidence and invents a figure. Compare process and outcome, then propose feedback.

**Worked solution.** A demonstrates the stated process criteria; explore why the audience disagreed and whether an assumption was missing. B fails critical accuracy and evidence criteria despite agreement; correct the unsupported figure and repeat the task. Do not average an ethical or factual breach away. The desired improvement is an observable action, not 'be more confident'.

Attempt before consulting the worked solution. Correct one specific reasoning gap and retry with an equivalent fictional case. Assess reasoning and safe boundaries, not the learner's own health or circumstances.

## Independent transfer

Use the scorecard for two fictional presentation outcomes, one favourable and one unfavourable. Assess the same criteria in both. Add a process improvement and identify evidence that would justify changing the criterion itself.
