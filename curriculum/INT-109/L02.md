# INT-109 / L02 — Testimony

**Draft — independent review pending.** Outcome: Compare and distinguish testimony in the context of epistemology.

## Orientation and retrieval

Retrieve the epistemic map. Much of what you know comes from other people rather than direct observation. When should a report change your belief?

## Core teaching

Testimony is information acquired through another person's assertion. It is essential because no individual can independently verify all relevant facts. Philosophers debate whether testimony has default justificatory force or requires supporting reasons about a speaker's reliability, among other positions. A practical evaluation can consider competence in the relevant domain, access to the information, sincerity, incentives, evidence provenance and corroboration. Expertise is domain-specific, not universal. Independent corroboration matters more than repeated copies of the same source. Do not dismiss a report merely because the speaker is unfamiliar; do not accept it merely because of status. The appropriate verification burden depends on consequences, plausibility and cost of checking. Directly consulting evidence is also fallible.

## Framework

Use the testimony chain. Identify the original observer or record; distinguish firsthand reports from relayed reports; assess domain competence and access; seek independent evidence; decide whether to accept provisionally, investigate or suspend judgement. Record uncertainty without demanding unnecessary private information.

## Worked example

Three fictional newsletters announce that a hall will close, all quoting the same anonymous post. This is one evidential chain, not three independent confirmations. The venue's official schedule and a separately obtained facilities notice may add genuinely different evidence, though they too could share a record. Verify date and scope before relying on the announcement.

### Contrasting case

A colleague tells you where an ordinary meeting is held and has direct access to the invitation. In a low-stakes context, demanding multiple independent sources for every routine statement is unnecessarily costly.

## Guided practice

Assess a fictional claim about a software outage from a user, a vendor status page and an article repeating that page. Model: user observation establishes their experience, vendor page describes the service, article adds no independent source. Compare geography and timing; withhold broad claims if scope is unknown.

Attempt before reading the model; compare your reasoning and correct a specific gap. Record assumptions and ask what would change your conclusion.

## Independent transfer

Draw a provenance tree for a public or fictional claim with at least three reports. Identify dependent sources, a missing original observation and a proportionate verification step.
