## Core teaching

Facilitation means helping a group use a process to achieve its intended purpose. It is different from deciding the outcome unless the facilitator also has actual decision authority. A good meeting distinguishes information sharing, consultation, deliberation and decision-making. Participants need to know which activity is occurring, what constraints apply and how their contributions will be used. A meeting without a clear output can become an extended discussion without ownership.

Prepare an agenda around outcomes rather than topics alone. Provide necessary information in advance, identify hard constraints and allow accessible ways to contribute. During discussion, separate the evidence from preferences, clarify ambiguous points and invite missing perspectives without forcing anyone to speak. Consider the trade-off between time and completeness: some issues can be resolved quickly, while a consequential uncertainty may need deferral. The most vocal person should not automatically determine the decision.

Finish by recording the decision or unresolved condition, action owners, deadlines and a review point. Distinguish an agreed commitment from a suggestion. If a matter exceeds the group's authority, record the required approval rather than presenting it as settled. Meeting effectiveness is demonstrated by accurate decisions and usable follow-through, not by unanimous enthusiasm. The methods here are original practical teaching tools informed by group decision-making research; they do not guarantee a productive meeting.

## Framework

**Outcome–agenda–contribute–decide–record**

State the meeting output and decision authority. Share the brief and time allocation. Check constraints and gather relevant contributions in accessible formats. Compare options by agreed criteria; decide under the actual authority or defer explicitly. Record the decision, actions, owners, dates and unresolved risks. Verify the record and circulate it only to appropriate recipients.

## Worked example

A fictional 30-minute venue meeting uses five minutes to confirm requirements, ten to compare three options, five to check uncertainties and ten to decide and assign actions. Option A costs less but its capacity is unverified; B meets confirmed requirements but costs more. If the group has authority to spend within its budget, it can select B or defer until A's capacity is verified. The recorder notes the chosen route, who will contact the venue and when confirmation is required. A participant unable to attend can supply relevant written information before the meeting. The output is an auditable decision rather than a discussion summary alone.

### Contrasting case

A facilitator announces a decision already made elsewhere but invites participants to vote as though their vote determines the result. This misrepresents authority. If the purpose is to explain or consult on implementation, state that honestly.

## Guided practice

**Design a 25-minute agenda.** Fictional objective: choose between two event dates and assign communications and booking. Both dates have known costs but one has an unresolved accessibility question. Specify outputs, time, decision authority and fallback.

**Worked solution.** Five minutes confirm requirements; eight compare dates and known costs; five address access; seven decide or assign a verification action and record owners. If access is a mandatory constraint and evidence is missing, defer commitment or select the verified feasible date under actual authority. Assign a communications owner and authorised booking owner. Record a deadline for confirmation and a review if the venue changes.

Now simulate one participant disagreeing with the preferred date. Ask which criterion the objection concerns and whether evidence changes feasibility. Do not require public agreement if the authorised process permits disagreement.

Attempt before checking the model. Identify a specific correction and repeat with an equivalent fictional case. A useful response states why the choice fits the circumstances, what remains uncertain and what would change the choice.
