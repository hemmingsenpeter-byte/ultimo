# INT-108 / L04 — Calibration

**Draft — independent review pending.** Outcome: Evaluate and justify decisions about calibration in the context of forecasting and decisions.

## Orientation and retrieval

Retrieve the difference between a probability forecast and a resolved event. If you assign 70% to many events, roughly how many should occur if those forecasts are calibrated?

## Core teaching

Calibration compares stated probabilities with observed frequencies across many forecasts. Among sufficiently comparable events assigned 70%, approximately 70% should occur for good calibration. A single outcome cannot establish calibration. Discrimination or resolution concerns whether forecasts distinguish events that occur from those that do not; assigning the same base rate to everything may be calibrated but uninformative. For binary events, a common Brier score convention is the mean of (p − o)², where o is 1 for yes and 0 for no. Lower is better; compare against a baseline on the same questions. Calibration bins with very few observations are noisy; report sample counts and avoid inferring permanent traits. Forecast updates should be dated, with a fixed scoring policy to prevent selecting only favourable predictions.

## Framework

Use a forecast ledger: question, initial forecast, timestamp, updates, resolution, scoring convention and comparison baseline. Compute individual Brier losses, then mean; group predictions into bins and compare mean probability with actual frequency. Inspect reasons for errors before adjusting your method.

## Worked example

Four fictional predictions are 0.8, 0.8, 0.3 and 0.3; outcomes are 1, 0, 0, 0. The losses are 0.04, 0.64, 0.09 and 0.09. Mean = 0.215. A constant 0.5 baseline scores 0.25 on these events. The higher-probability bin resolves 1/2, the lower bin 0/2. With only two observations per bin, do not conclude the forecaster is systematically overconfident.

### Contrasting case

A correct 99% forecast receives a small loss, but one such success is not evidence of reliable 99% confidence. Extreme confidence needs substantial support and evaluation over time.

## Guided practice

Calculate losses for forecasts 0.6, 0.4, 0.9 with outcomes 1, 0, 0. Model: 0.16, 0.16, 0.81; mean 1.13/3 ≈ 0.377. Compare the 0.25 baseline and identify which forecast contributes most. Do not revise earlier probabilities after seeing outcomes.

Attempt before reading the model; compare your reasoning and correct a specific gap. Record assumptions and ask what would change your conclusion.
