# INT-108 / L01 — Resolvable forecasts

**Draft — independent review pending.** Outcome: Explain resolvable forecasts in the context of forecasting and decisions.

## Orientation and retrieval

Recall why a hypothesis needs an observable test. What is wrong with a prediction that a new service will soon be successful?

## Core teaching

A useful probabilistic forecast identifies a future event whose outcome can be determined by an agreed rule. A binary event resolves yes or no; a numeric forecast needs a specified measure and date. A good question defines event, population, threshold, deadline, time zone where relevant, source of resolution and a rule for unavailable or revised data. A probability describes uncertainty before resolution, not a promise. Record the forecast and its information date. Distinguish an event date from the date when evidence becomes available. Avoid vague adjectives, hidden conjunctions and outcomes that the forecaster can manipulate. Define conditional questions carefully: if the condition never occurs, the conditional forecast may be void rather than false.

## Framework

Use EVENT: event and threshold; verification source; expiry and time zone; null or ambiguous resolution rule; timestamped probability. Have another learner apply the rule to three possible outcomes before forecasting.

## Worked example

Instead of 'the fictional club will grow quickly', use 'Will the club register at least 120 active members by 31 March at 17:00 local time, as recorded in its final register for that date?' Define active and duplicates in advance, and void the question if the register is unavailable by a stated verification deadline. A forecast of 65% is a judgement, not an observed frequency.

### Contrasting case

'Will the club be popular?' cannot be consistently resolved: different evaluators may use attendance, satisfaction or public recognition. A measurable proxy may help but is not identical to popularity.

## Guided practice

Rewrite 'the refurbishment will finish soon'. Model: 'Will the fictional project's independent completion certificate be dated on or before 30 June?' Specify what counts as the certificate, the source and the data-absence rule. Then write three hypothetical records: timely, late and missing; classify each.

Attempt before reading the model; compare your reasoning and correct a specific gap. Record assumptions and ask what would change your conclusion.

## Independent transfer

Write five low-stakes forecasting questions about fictional or publicly verifiable events. Include one numeric outcome, one binary event and one conditional question. Define resolution rules before assigning probabilities.

## Knowledge check

1. What is a resolution criterion?
2. Why timestamp a prediction?
3. Does missing resolution evidence necessarily imply no?
