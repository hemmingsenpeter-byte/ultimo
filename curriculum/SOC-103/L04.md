# SOC-103 / L04 — Repair

**Draft — independent academic and qualified subject review pending.** Outcome: Evaluate and justify decisions about repair in the context of friendship and community.

## Orientation and retrieval

Retrieve EMO-104's repair framework. A fictional community member forgets an agreed contribution and the event is disrupted. What does effective repair require beyond an apology?

## Core teaching

Friendship and community repair involves addressing an identifiable breach or misunderstanding while respecting the participants' autonomy. A missed commitment, incorrect communication or unintentional exclusion may have practical consequences. The appropriate response depends on evidence of the issue, the actual role and what can be corrected. A conflict does not automatically mean a relationship should end or that everyone must reconcile.

Separate the behaviour, impact, responsibility and future arrangement. A repair may include acknowledging a breach, correcting information, restoring a resource, adjusting a process or communicating an authorised next step. Research on apologies examines components associated with perceived effectiveness in specific scenarios; it does not establish a guaranteed method of restoring trust. If responsibility is shared or disputed, clarify the facts fairly instead of forcing one person to apologise for everything.

Community repair may also require a change to group design. If newcomers are repeatedly excluded because event information is inaccessible, an individual apology alone will not address the barrier. A revised communication process may be more useful. Where misconduct, discrimination or safety issues arise, follow appropriate procedures and seek qualified or organisational support. Do not insist that someone who has experienced harm participate in a reconciliation conversation. The learner's role is to propose safe and feasible repair, not compel forgiveness or measure popularity.

## Framework

**Name–listen–correct–prevent–review**

Describe the specific incident and its impact. Invite relevant clarification if safe and wanted. Accept justified responsibility and identify a feasible correction. Change a process or commitment to reduce recurrence. Agree a review point and respect the decision not to engage. If the issue exceeds authority or involves danger, use appropriate formal support. Text model: evidence → responsibility → practical correction → observable follow-through.

## Worked example

A fictional volunteer sends the wrong meeting time to newcomers but the regular members receive the correction in a private group. Two newcomers arrive after the activity ends. Repair involves acknowledging the communication error, providing accurate future information and correcting the process so all participants receive changes through the agreed channel. The organiser may offer another session if feasible, but cannot promise compensation without authority. If a newcomer does not want further contact, respect that decision. A later check confirms that event updates reach the appropriate list; the measure is corrected access, not whether everyone becomes friends.

### Contrasting case

A participant repeatedly insults another member, apologises and then continues. An apology does not remove the need to address ongoing conduct. The group may need an authorised conduct process and appropriate support. Do not pressure the affected person to attend a private repair meeting.

## Guided practice

**Compare responses to a fictional missed booking.** A: 'Sorry everyone got upset; these things happen.' B: 'I didn't confirm the room booking, so the group couldn't meet. I'll check available dates with the coordinator and confirm future bookings in the shared schedule.' Identify strengths, gaps and review criteria.

**Worked solution.** A acknowledges upset but avoids the concrete issue and correction. B names the omission, impact and proposed prevention. It should verify authority and check whether other participants need a revised arrangement. A review could confirm the next booking and public schedule before invitations are sent. Do not demand acceptance of the apology.

Now vary the facts: the venue cancelled unexpectedly after a confirmed booking. Responsibility for the cancellation differs; the group may still benefit from a contingency communication plan. Revise the response without inventing blame.

Attempt before checking the worked solution. Identify a specific reasoning or communication correction and repeat using an equivalent fictional case. Assess observable task quality, not personality, popularity or another person's compliance.
