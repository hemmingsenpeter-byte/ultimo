# SOC-103 / L02 — Reciprocity

**Draft — independent academic and qualified subject review pending.** Outcome: Compare and distinguish reciprocity in the context of friendship and community.

## Orientation and retrieval

Retrieve the distinction between opportunity and mutual closeness. A fictional friend often provides practical help while another provides conversation and companionship. Is reciprocity the same as matching every contribution exactly?

## Core teaching

Reciprocity concerns mutual participation, responsiveness and consideration over time. It is not necessarily an exact exchange of identical actions, equal financial expenditure or a ledger of favours. Friends may offer different kinds of help: emotional support, practical assistance, information or companionship. Capacity can vary with circumstances. A relationship may be reciprocal even when one person needs more support during a difficult period, provided the arrangement respects choice and realistic limits.

Distinguish voluntary generosity from an expectation of repayment that was never agreed. An offer does not entitle the giver to private information, affection, invitations or another favour. Conversely, repeated unacknowledged commitments or pressure to provide assistance may justify a discussion about feasibility. Look at actual agreements and patterns rather than using a vague score of friendship quality. One unanswered message is not proof of exploitation; repeated breaches of an explicit commitment are a different kind of evidence.

Reciprocity also involves making and receiving requests appropriately. People may decline help, prefer another source or contribute in a different way. The aim is a sustainable relationship rather than perfect symmetry. Social support research distinguishes types and functions of support; this lesson uses those concepts to build a practical comparison, not a validated measure of friendship. Where coercion or threats occur, seek appropriate assistance rather than negotiating a more equal exchange.

## Framework

**Offer–receive–acknowledge–review**

Identify what is offered, whether it is wanted and whether any obligation was actually agreed. Consider the available capacity and different forms of contribution. Express appreciation or clarify expectations without creating an invented debt. If an arrangement repeatedly fails, identify the specific impact and propose a realistic change. Respect refusal, role boundaries and privacy. Text model: mutual choice + feasible commitments + respectful response, not numerical balance.

## Worked example

A fictional friend drives another to a community event several times. The passenger helps with planning and shares useful information. The driver later finds the trips difficult to sustain. Rather than decide the passenger is selfish because the contributions differ, they clarify availability and ask whether another transport arrangement would work. If cost-sharing was previously agreed and not fulfilled, address that specific agreement. Otherwise, past voluntary offers do not create an undisclosed debt. Both may choose a different arrangement and continue the friendship.

### Contrasting case

A person buys an acquaintance an expensive gift and subsequently demands an invitation to a private event. The gift does not create an obligation to provide access or closeness. The recipient can decline, and coercive repayment demands are not healthy reciprocity.

## Guided practice

**Compare three fictional patterns.** A: friends exchange different kinds of voluntary support. B: a person repeatedly fails to complete an agreed shared task. C: someone gives unsolicited help and later demands a favour. Identify which concerns unequal contribution, an actual breach or an invented obligation.

**Worked solution.** A can be mutually satisfactory despite unequal hours. B warrants discussion of the specific commitment, impact and alternative. C does not establish a debt because the repayment was not agreed. For B, draft a request: 'The bookings were due yesterday and I couldn't confirm the event. Can we agree a feasible division of tasks?' Do not infer character from a single missed commitment.

Now vary A: one friend can no longer offer the same help. A respectful response revises the arrangement rather than punishing reduced capacity. Identify another support source if necessary.

Attempt before checking the worked solution. Identify a specific reasoning or communication correction and repeat using an equivalent fictional case. Assess observable task quality, not personality, popularity or another person's compliance.
