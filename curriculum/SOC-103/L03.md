# SOC-103 / L03 — Inclusive invitations

**Draft — independent academic and qualified subject review pending.** Outcome: Apply inclusive invitations in the context of friendship and community.

## Orientation and retrieval

Recall the recurring-contact plan and EMO-106 on requests. A fictional organiser wants to invite newcomers to a group without pressuring them or making participation inaccessible. What information should the invitation contain?

## Core teaching

An inclusive invitation communicates a real opportunity in a way that makes the relevant conditions understandable and allows an appropriate choice. It states the activity, timing, location or format, likely cost, participation expectations and how to accept or decline. Depending on the setting, accessibility information and a contact for questions can help. An invitation is not a demand for participation or a test of loyalty.

Distinguish inclusion from assuming that everyone wants the same activity. A group may have legitimate capacity or role restrictions; make those transparent and apply fair procedures. Do not promise accessibility features that have not been confirmed. Avoid making people disclose health, finances or cultural identity merely to request an alternative. If an accommodation requires an authorised process, explain the route without soliciting unnecessary private information.

Low-pressure language makes it easier to decline. An organiser may need an RSVP by a specific date, but silence does not establish consent. A follow-up can be appropriate when logistics require it; repeated persuasion after refusal is not necessary. Existing members can help orient newcomers by explaining actual group conventions and inviting participation without demanding attention, personal stories or a particular social style. This is an original design framework, grounded in respect for autonomy and accessibility rather than a claim that one invitation format creates friendship.

## Framework

**Purpose–details–access–choice**

Name the activity and who it is for. Specify time, place or channel, cost and what participation involves. State confirmed access conditions and a contact for questions. Make the choice and RSVP process clear; provide an easy way to decline without explanation. Identify what happens if capacity is reached or plans change. For an ordinary invitation, acceptance must be voluntary.

Text example: activity + logistics + access information + optional response.

## Worked example

A fictional community group plans a Saturday sketch session. The organiser writes: 'We're meeting at the library meeting room from 10 to 11:30 for an informal sketch session. Beginners are welcome; bring any materials you already have. There is no attendance fee. Please let me know by Thursday if you'd like a place. If you need information about access, contact the organiser; we will confirm the venue details. No problem if you can't make it.' The invitation provides useful facts without promising unverified access or asking why someone declines. If the room has a capacity limit, state how places are allocated.

### Contrasting case

An invitation says, 'Real friends will be there; if you decline, explain why.' This uses social pressure and requires unnecessary disclosure. It is not made inclusive merely by being addressed to a large group.

## Guided practice

**Revise a fictional invitation.** Original: 'Everyone come to the usual place Friday. Don't be boring. Bring money.' Identify missing information and coercive wording. Draft a better invitation under the given facts: a 6 pm board-game meeting, public venue, optional shared snack cost, and RSVP requested by Wednesday.

**Worked solution.** 'We're holding a board-game meeting on Friday at 6 pm at the community room. Joining is free; bringing or buying a snack is optional. Let us know by Wednesday if you'd like to attend so we can check capacity. Contact the organiser for access questions; we'll confirm the details. You're welcome to decline.' This makes the activity and choice clear, and separates optional costs from attendance.

Now consider a newcomer who prefers written instructions. Offer a brief description of the games or a quiet participation option where feasible. Do not demand a reason for the preference or assume a disability.

Attempt before checking the worked solution. Identify a specific reasoning or communication correction and repeat using an equivalent fictional case. Assess observable task quality, not personality, popularity or another person's compliance.

## Independent transfer

Design two fictional inclusive invitations: one recurring community activity and one one-off social gathering. Include confirmed logistics, access uncertainty, cost, RSVP, an opt-out and an appropriate follow-up rule. Compare how the format changes with context.
