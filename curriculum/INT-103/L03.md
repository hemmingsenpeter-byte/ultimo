# INT-103 / L03 — Sampling bias

**Draft — independent review pending.** Outcome: Apply sampling bias in the context of probability and statistics.

## Orientation and retrieval

Before reading, attempt the first knowledge check without notes. Recall Descriptive statistics and connect it to this topic.

## Core teaching

A population is the group about which a claim is made; a sample is the subset observed. Random sampling can reduce systematic selection distortion when the sampling frame is appropriate. Convenience samples can differ from the target group. Larger samples reduce some random variation but do not repair systematic exclusion or nonresponse. A survey's question wording, collection method and target population all matter. Sampling and causal experimental assignment are different design choices.

## Framework

Define population → inspect frame → recruitment → nonresponse → measure → qualify inference.

This sequence is an instructional model, not a universal law. Check its limitations against the contrasting case.

## Worked example

A gym surveys 500 members about exercise and reports that most adults train weekly. Gym membership selects people likely to exercise. The survey may describe respondents, but national inference is weak.

### Contrasting case

A small randomly selected sample from a complete population frame may have larger random uncertainty yet less systematic selection bias.

## Guided practice

Critique a survey of 1,000 app users claiming national satisfaction. Model: app users exclude nonusers; voluntary response may attract extremes; report respondent satisfaction or obtain a broader frame.

Attempt the exercise before comparing to the model answer. Correct one specific gap.

## Independent transfer

Design a simple fictional survey of neighbourhood transport preferences with a recruitment and nonresponse plan.
