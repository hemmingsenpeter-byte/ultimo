# INT-107 / L03 — Graph interpretation

**Draft — independent review pending.** Outcome: Apply graph interpretation in the context of mathematics and science literacy.

## Orientation and retrieval

Recall the difference between a stock and its change. When looking at a chart, how would you distinguish a rising level from a rising rate of growth?

## Core teaching

Graphs encode quantities through positions, lengths, areas and scales. Begin with the axes, units, time interval, population and data source. For a line graph, slope represents change in vertical quantity per horizontal unit, not simply its level. A rising line with a decreasing slope still rises, but more slowly. Bars commonly encode magnitudes through length and generally need a meaningful zero baseline; line charts may use a truncated axis to reveal variation if clearly labelled. Logarithmic scales encode multiplicative differences. Histograms group numeric observations into intervals; bin width changes the apparent shape, and unequal widths require care with frequency density. A smooth line between observations may imply interpolation that was not measured. A graph alone cannot establish causal direction.

## Framework

Use AXIS: axes and units; extent and scale; intervals and missing values; statement supported. Then write two descriptions: what the data visibly show and what they do not establish. Convert a graph back into a small table if visual presentation obscures the comparison.

## Worked example

A fictional venue records attendance of 100, 120, 130 and 135 over four months. Levels rise; changes fall from 20 to 10 to 5. A line drawn on a vertical axis beginning at 95 may exaggerate perceived magnitude but still accurately reveal changes if labelled. A bar display starting at 95 would make differences in bar lengths disproportionate to actual attendance ratios.

### Contrasting case

Two independent time series rising together do not establish that one caused the other. A shared seasonal pattern could explain both.

## Guided practice

Sketch attendance values 80, 100, 90 and 110. Describe overall change (+30) and interval changes (+20, −10, +20). Explain why a chart omitting month three would hide the decline. Provide a text table as the accessible alternative.

## Independent transfer

Compare two representations of the same fictional dataset, one using a zero baseline and one zoomed. State the question each answers, and propose a clearer label for the scale.
