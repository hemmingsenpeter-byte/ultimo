# INT-107 / L01 — Units and ratios

**Draft — independent review pending.** Outcome: Explain units and ratios in the context of mathematics and science literacy.

## Orientation and retrieval

Without a calculator, decide whether 600 metres in two minutes is closer to 3 or 300 metres per second. What does the denominator represent?

## Core teaching

A quantity combines a number with a unit. A ratio compares quantities; a rate usually relates quantities with different units, such as kilometres per hour. A conversion changes representation without changing the underlying quantity. Multiply by a conversion factor equal to one, arranged so unwanted units cancel. Dimensional analysis checks that units on both sides of an equation agree. It can detect many errors but cannot identify a wrong numerical factor or prove a model. Distinguish percentages (parts per hundred), percentage points (differences between percentages), and relative changes (difference divided by baseline). Square and cubic conversions require squaring or cubing the length conversion factor. Report appropriate precision rather than implying measurements are exact.

## Framework

Write the target unit, identify starting quantities, arrange conversion fractions, cancel units, calculate and sense-check magnitude. For ratios, state numerator, denominator and comparison population. Example text layout: 600 m / 2 min × 1 min / 60 s = 5 m/s.

## Worked example

A fictional room measures 4 m by 3 m. Area is 12 m². Because 1 m = 100 cm, 1 m² = 10,000 cm² and the area is 120,000 cm². A quote of $240 for the room is $20/m². If the area is rounded, the unit price is also approximate.

### Contrasting case

Converting 12 m² into 1,200 cm² applies a linear factor to an area. Both the magnitude and the unit relationship are wrong.

## Guided practice

Calculate 1.5 km in 20 minutes as km/h and m/s. Model: 1.5 × 3 = 4.5 km/h, equivalent to 4,500/3,600 = 1.25 m/s. Separately, an error rate rises from 4% to 6%: 2 percentage points, 50% relative increase. Explain why both descriptions are correct.

Attempt before reading the model; compare your reasoning and correct a specific gap. Record assumptions and ask what would change your conclusion.

## Independent transfer

Create three conversion problems involving length, area and a rate. Include one intentionally incorrect solution and explain which check exposes it.

## Knowledge check

2. How many cm² are in one m²?
3. Is a change from 10% to 15% a 5% relative increase?
