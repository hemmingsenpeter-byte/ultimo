# MEN-102 / L03 — Protected practice

**Draft — independent academic and qualified subject review pending.** Outcome: Apply protected practice in the context of attention and concentration.

## Orientation and retrieval

Recall the three interruption categories. Suppose a learner has forty minutes available but no clear deliverable. What would count as a successful practice session, beyond having remained seated?

## Core teaching

Protected practice is a deliberately arranged opportunity to work on a chosen task with fewer unnecessary competing demands. It is not a demand to maintain perfect concentration. The session begins with an observable outcome: solve three representative problems and check the reasoning, draft one section with evidence, or practise a skill safely. Without a defined task, a timer measures exposure rather than learning. Retrieval and feedback from INT-101 provide better evidence of learning than elapsed time.

Useful design features include a clear cue to start, materials ready, a realistic boundary, an agreed interruption policy, a resumption aid and an exit condition. An if–then plan connects a predictable situation to an intended response. A session length is a trial parameter, not a biological prescription: adjust it for task demands, access needs and recovery. Switching research supports taking unnecessary task changes seriously, but does not establish one optimal focus schedule. The particular planning sequence below is original curriculum design.

Track at least one output-quality measure and one resource measure, such as effort or time. A practice block can be successful even if the learner discovers they need clarification or rest. The method fails when it creates unsafe unavailability, hides accessibility barriers, or becomes a perfectionistic ritual. Protected time also depends on working conditions and caring responsibilities; organisational support may be necessary. The plan should permit interruption for safety and essential duties.

## Framework

**Prepare–perform–verify–adapt**

Prepare: select one observable result and gather materials. Agree a safe contact method and choose a manageable time window. Perform: work on the task, capture unrelated items and resume after necessary switches. Verify: check output against an explicit criterion without counting time as competence. Adapt: choose the next task or end the session; modify one variable next time.

Worksheet fields: result; first action; resources; response policy; exit condition; quality measure; recovery need. The worksheet is an instructional aid, not a prescribed treatment.

## Worked example

A fictional learner wants to study logic. Their first plan is 'study for 45 minutes'. They revise it to 'identify the premises and conclusion in three unfamiliar arguments and explain one missing bridge'. They arrange a short session around an agreed availability window. On completion, two maps are correct; the third confuses a premise with the conclusion. The session has identified a specific learning gap. A follow-up session uses a new example rather than repeating the same answer. The learner stops at the agreed time instead of extending the session until all mistakes disappear.

### Contrasting case

A learner with frequent caregiving duties may have five-minute windows rather than a long uninterrupted period. A small task with a clear resumption note is a legitimate adaptation. A long block is not inherently superior if it cannot be safely or realistically protected.

## Guided practice

### Design two session variants

Use the fictional objective: review four customer feedback summaries and group recurring themes. Variant A has a single 20-minute window. Variant B has two ten-minute windows with an unavoidable handover in between. Specify the first action, output criterion, resumption cue and urgent contact arrangement for each.

### Worked solution

A: read each summary, mark candidate themes, verify at least one example per theme. B: use the first window for two summaries; record their theme labels and next document; use the second to complete and compare. Both produce a checked theme table. Their suitability depends on task continuity and actual availability; neither is automatically better. Evaluate accuracy and completion, not conformity to the timer.

Attempt the task before checking the worked solution. Identify one justified correction, then repeat using an equivalent fictional case. The criterion is quality of reasoning, not personal performance or symptom change.

## Independent transfer

Plan one low-risk learning session and an alternative version for a disrupted schedule. Complete either a fictional simulation or actual non-sensitive task. Record the output criterion, an observed gap, and a single design change for the next session.
