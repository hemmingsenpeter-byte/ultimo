# MEN-102 / L02 — Interruptions

**Draft — independent academic and qualified subject review pending.** Outcome: Compare and distinguish interruptions in the context of attention and concentration.

## Orientation and retrieval

Retrieve the task–load–support map. In a fictional office, a phone alarm, a colleague's urgent request and an impulse to check a different document all break concentration. Which interruptions must be handled immediately, and which can be postponed?

## Core teaching

An interruption is an event that suspends an ongoing task; a distraction can divert attention without a full task switch. Interruptions may originate externally, such as an alert, or internally, such as a remembered obligation. A task switch requires reconfiguring the active rule and recovering the relevant context. Experimental switching costs vary with rule complexity and cues. They should not be converted into a universal claim that every notification wastes a fixed amount of time.

Leroy's work examines the possibility that part of one's attention remains engaged with a previous task after moving to another. This is often described as attention residue. Unfinished work may therefore matter when designing transitions. A resumption cue, such as a brief written next step, is a reasonable operational aid to try; its value should be assessed in the relevant setting, rather than treated as a guaranteed intervention. Notifications that convey an important safety or caregiving obligation are not inherently harmful. The objective is reliable prioritisation.

Separate interruptions by urgency, consequence of delay and whether they require a new task or merely acknowledgement. A blanket instruction to silence everything ignores legitimate responsibilities. Likewise, responding to every incoming message immediately can undermine tasks that depend on continuity. Team-level arrangements, such as agreed escalation channels and predictable response windows, may be more effective than asking a single worker to resist all cues. Do not impose an arrangement on others without agreement.

## Framework

### Capture–triage–resume

- Capture the request briefly without opening unrelated work if it is safe to defer.
- Triage: immediate safety/essential duty, time-sensitive commitment, or deferrable item.
- For an unavoidable switch, note the current goal, last completed step and next action.
- Resume using the cue; verify the last completed step before continuing.
- Review which interruptions were avoidable and which arrangements require agreement.

Text example: current task → interruption → triage → handle or schedule → verified resumption. This is an original workflow, not a clinically tested attention protocol.

## Worked example

A fictional analyst is checking a ten-row spreadsheet. At row six, a teammate asks for routine information that is due tomorrow. The analyst captures the request and plans a reply after the check. Later, a building emergency notification requires immediate action. They stop safely, leave a short note that rows one to five are verified and row six is incomplete, and follow emergency procedures. When it is appropriate to return, they verify row six before moving on. The relevant quality measure is accurate completion and responsiveness to the urgent event, not uninterrupted minutes alone.

### Contrasting case

A dispatcher cannot defer an urgent call just because it disrupts a preferred work block. The job is inherently responsive. A better intervention might involve explicit transfer of responsibility or a standard resumption checklist, designed with the team.

## Guided practice

### Triage a fictional interruption log

At 10:10 a routine newsletter arrives; at 10:12 a colleague needs a figure by 15:00; at 10:14 a safety alert is issued; at 10:30 a learner remembers a shopping item. Assign each to immediate action, scheduled response or capture only. Draft a resumption note for a spreadsheet whose next unchecked line is 12.

### Worked solution

Newsletter: defer or discard; colleague: acknowledge or schedule before the agreed deadline if appropriate; safety alert: follow the relevant emergency procedure immediately; shopping item: capture for later. Resumption note: 'Rows 1–11 verified; row 12 not checked; compare quantity and unit price, then continue.' Feedback: a successful answer preserves urgent obligations, avoids storing sensitive material and specifies the next observable action.

Attempt the task before checking the worked solution. Identify one justified correction, then repeat using an equivalent fictional case. The criterion is quality of reasoning, not personal performance or symptom change.

## Independent transfer

Design a five-entry simulated interruption log for a different work or learning context. Distinguish source, urgency, response and resumption cost; propose one team-level change and one individual change. Explain what would make either inappropriate.
