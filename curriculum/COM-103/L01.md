## Core teaching

Reflective listening checks meaning rather than simply repeating words. A paraphrase restates content in your own language; a reflection may tentatively identify the speaker's concern. Active-listening research in initial interactions compares such responses with advice and acknowledgements, but results are context-specific. Listening is not agreement, therapy or a guarantee of rapport. The speaker retains authority to correct your interpretation.

## Framework

Attend → paraphrase → check → allow correction. Use a question such as 'Have I understood that correctly?'

This sequence is an instructional model, not a universal law. Check its limitations against the contrasting case.

## Worked example

A colleague says, 'Every task arrives at the last minute.' Reply: 'It sounds as though unpredictable deadlines make planning difficult. Is that the issue?' They clarify that the missing specifications are the real problem.

### Contrasting case

'You are obviously anxious' assigns an internal state without checking; unsolicited advice may miss the concern.

## Guided practice

Paraphrase 'I have too many meetings to finish this report.' Model: 'The meetings are leaving you little uninterrupted time; is that right?' Ask one clarification.

Attempt the exercise before comparing to the model answer. Correct one specific gap.

## Independent transfer

Role-play with a consenting partner or write both sides of a fictional exchange, including a correction.
