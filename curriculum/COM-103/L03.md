# COM-103 / L03 — Fair summary

**Draft — independent review pending.** Outcome: Apply fair summary in the context of listening and questioning.

## Orientation and retrieval

Before reading, attempt the first knowledge check without notes. Recall Question types and connect it to this topic.

## Core teaching

A fair summary distinguishes established facts, each participant's position, areas of agreement and unresolved issues. It should represent viewpoints in terms their holders could recognise, without implying all claims have equal evidential weight. Separate interpretation from quotation, and ask participants to correct the summary. This is an educational meeting technique; it does not resolve every conflict or replace formal mediation.

## Framework

Facts → perspectives → agreement → uncertainty → next step → confirmation.

This sequence is an instructional model, not a universal law. Check its limitations against the contrasting case.

## Worked example

Two colleagues disagree on launching a product. One prioritises a deadline; the other worries about test coverage. Both want a reliable release. A fair summary records both aims, the test uncertainty and a proposed decision meeting.

### Contrasting case

'One person cares about quality and the other cares about money' imputes motives and distorts both positions.

## Guided practice

Summarise a fictional dispute about office hours in four sentences. Model: state the proposal, each concern, shared goal and outstanding data.

Attempt the exercise before comparing to the model answer. Correct one specific gap.

## Independent transfer

Summarise a different disagreement, then write the strongest correction each side might make.

## Knowledge check

1. Does fair summary require endorsing both claims?
2. What should uncertain facts be called?
3. Who should be invited to correct the summary?

1. No.
2. Unresolved or unverified.
3. The people whose views are represented.
