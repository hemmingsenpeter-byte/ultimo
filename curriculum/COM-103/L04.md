## Core teaching

Consent-based questioning protects privacy and agency. People may choose not to discuss health, money, relationships or other sensitive topics. A question should have a legitimate purpose, an appropriate context and a genuine opportunity to decline. Power differences can make a nominal choice feel compulsory; clarify whether answering is optional and avoid penalties for refusal. Listening also includes respecting silence.

## Framework

Purpose → permission → neutral question → accept decline → record only necessary information.

This sequence is an instructional model, not a universal law. Check its limitations against the contrasting case.

## Worked example

A team leader asks whether a colleague wants to discuss workload and says they need not share personal reasons. If the colleague declines, they agree a practical handover plan without requesting medical details.

### Contrasting case

Asking for intimate details in a compulsory team session and calling it voluntary ignores the power imbalance.

## Guided practice

Rewrite a fictional intrusive question about why someone missed an event. Model: 'Would you like to discuss whether the schedule needs changing? You don't have to explain personal circumstances.'

Attempt the exercise before comparing to the model answer. Correct one specific gap.
