# COM-103 / L02 — Question types

**Draft — independent review pending.** Outcome: Compare and distinguish question types in the context of listening and questioning.

## Orientation and retrieval

Before reading, attempt the first knowledge check without notes. Recall Reflective listening and connect it to this topic.

## Core teaching

Open questions invite elaboration; closed questions seek a bounded answer. Both are useful. Leading questions suggest the desired answer, while loaded questions embed an assumption. Questions can help people explain their view, but an interrogation can pressure them. Use fewer, relevant questions and allow the speaker to decline. In a sensitive context, ask permission before changing topic.

## Framework

Clarify purpose → choose open or closed → remove assumptions → ask one question → listen.

This sequence is an instructional model, not a universal law. Check its limitations against the contrasting case.

## Worked example

To understand a delayed project, ask 'What changed since the plan was agreed?' Then confirm 'Is the supplier delivery expected Friday?' The first gathers context; the second checks a detail.

### Contrasting case

'Why did you fail to plan properly?' presupposes fault and may discourage accurate explanation.

## Guided practice

Rewrite 'You didn't care about the deadline, did you?' Model: 'What got in the way of meeting the deadline?' Then ask one closed follow-up to confirm an agreed fact.

Attempt the exercise before comparing to the model answer. Correct one specific gap.

## Independent transfer

Design five questions for a fictional interview; label each open, closed or leading and revise the leading one.

## Knowledge check

1. When is a closed question useful?
2. What is a leading question?
3. Why ask permission on sensitive topics?
