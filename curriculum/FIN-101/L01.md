# FIN-101 / L01 — Financial stocks and flows

## Core teaching

A financial stock is measured at a point in time, such as savings or a loan balance. A flow is measured over a period, such as monthly income or expenses. Net worth is assets minus liabilities; cash surplus is income minus spending over a defined period. Positive net worth does not guarantee liquid cash, and a positive monthly surplus does not erase outstanding debt. Use consistent dates, currencies and periods. This course uses fictional NZD figures for literacy, not personalised advice.

## Framework

Snapshot assets/liabilities → calculate net worth → list periodic flows → calculate surplus → reconcile changes.

This sequence is an instructional model, not a universal law. Check its limitations against the contrasting case.

## Worked example

A fictional household has $12,000 cash, a $20,000 car and $15,000 debt: net worth $17,000. Monthly income $4,000 and expenses $3,700 yield $300 surplus. The car is an asset but not immediately spendable cash.

### Contrasting case

Counting the entire $20,000 car value as monthly income confuses a stock with a flow.

## Guided practice
