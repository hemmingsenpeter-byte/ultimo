# FIN-101 / L02 — Budget and balance sheet

## Core teaching

A budget is a spending plan that assigns income to expenses, saving and other priorities. Start from after-tax income for cash planning, distinguish committed and discretionary costs, and include irregular items. A balance sheet separately lists what is owned and owed at a specific date. For a budget, subtract planned outflows from inflows; a deficit calls for review of options, not a moral judgement. Some expenses cannot be reduced without harm. Financial hardship may warrant free independent support.

## Framework

Define period → list net income → categorise costs → allocate priorities → subtract → review.

This sequence is an instructional model, not a universal law. Check its limitations against the contrasting case.

## Worked example

Monthly income $4,500; rent $1,800; bills $450; food $650; transport $400; debt payments $300; leisure $250; saving $200. Total $4,050, leaving $450 unallocated. Decide what the remainder must cover before treating it as spare cash.

### Contrasting case

A budget that ignores food or annual insurance can appear balanced while hiding real expenses.

## Guided practice

Prepare a fictional budget using $3,800 income and costs of $1,500, $400, $600, $350, $200 and $300. Model: total $3,350; remaining $450. Identify an omitted category.

Attempt the exercise before comparing to the model answer. Correct one specific gap.

## Knowledge check

3. What is the difference between budget and balance sheet?
