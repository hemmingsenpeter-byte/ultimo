## Core teaching

Irregular expenses occur less frequently than the budget period. Annual insurance, repairs or gifts may be predictable in amount or timing even when they do not recur monthly. A sinking-fund allocation spreads an expected cost across periods; an emergency buffer addresses uncertainty. Do not count the same saving transfer and later spending twice when consolidating accounts. Distinguish planned allocations from actual expenses and reconcile monthly.

## Framework

List irregular costs → estimate annual amount → divide by periods → reserve → compare actuals.

This sequence is an instructional model, not a universal law. Check its limitations against the contrasting case.

## Worked example

Annual vehicle costs of $1,200 and insurance of $960 imply monthly allocations of $100 and $80. If both are reserved monthly, $180 is allocated before the bills arrive. Actual costs can differ.

### Contrasting case

An unexpected urgent repair is not identical to a predictable annual premium; both need planning, but their uncertainty differs.
