## Core teaching

Stress testing asks how a budget behaves when assumptions change. Construct plausible scenarios rather than predicting one future: lower income, higher essential costs, or a one-off expense. Calculate the new surplus, identify which obligations are fixed, and consider safe options such as support or a revised plan. A financial buffer can absorb some shocks, but it is finite. Use scenarios to reveal fragility, not to prescribe investment or debt decisions.

## Framework

Baseline → change assumptions → recompute → identify gap → compare options → set review trigger.

This sequence is an instructional model, not a universal law. Check its limitations against the contrasting case.

## Worked example

Baseline monthly surplus $400. A $250 income reduction and $200 increase in essential costs produce a $50 deficit. A $600 one-off bill would create a $650 gap that month if not already reserved. The household must review options and support.

### Contrasting case

Assuming a credit card automatically solves a recurring deficit ignores future repayments and cost.

## Guided practice

For income $4,000, expenses $3,600, test a $300 income reduction and $150 higher costs. Model: income $3,700 minus expenses $3,750 equals -$50.

Attempt the exercise before comparing to the model answer. Correct one specific gap.
