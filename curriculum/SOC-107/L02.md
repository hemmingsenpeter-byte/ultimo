## Core teaching

Presentation concerns how a person or message is made available to others. It may include clothing, grooming, organisation, materials, language, voice and communication format. Its practical value depends on context. Appropriate protective clothing can be essential in a hazardous workplace; clean and suitable clothing may be helpful for an ordinary event; a legible document may be more important than formal dress in an online meeting. Do not equate expense, attractiveness, accent, body type or conventional style with competence or moral worth.

Separate functional suitability, communication quality and impression. Functional suitability asks whether the format or clothing meets actual task, safety and access requirements. Communication quality concerns accuracy, organisation and clarity. Impression concerns how observers interpret those features, which may be biased or inaccurate. Social perception research distinguishes perceived warmth and competence, but perceived competence is not proof of actual ability. Professional presentation should support truthful communication, not create misleading credentials or conceal relevant limitations.

Preparation can reduce preventable barriers: check the event purpose, instructions, necessary equipment and information, and make a short introduction suited to the role. Respect individual cultural, religious, disability-related and personal preferences. Where a rule is unclear or potentially exclusionary, seek clarification or an appropriate accommodation. Do not prescribe grooming routines, judge someone's body or require an unnecessary personal image. The assessment concerns fit with a legitimate task, accurate content and accessibility rather than attractiveness or conformity.

## Framework

**Task–requirements–materials–message–check**

Define the intended activity. Verify actual clothing, equipment, access or format requirements. Prepare accurate information and a usable communication format. Choose presentation options compatible with the setting and personal preference. Check whether any statement implies unsupported expertise or authority. Confirm readiness and identify a contingency.

Text model: functional fit + accurate content + accessibility → suitable presentation; impression alone is not evidence.

## Worked example

A fictional learner is attending a professional event with a short site tour. The organiser specifies closed footwear and provided protective equipment for the tour, and otherwise gives no dress code. The learner checks the actual requirements, prepares a concise description of their experience and brings accessible notes. They do not buy expensive clothing solely to signal status. During the discussion, they distinguish tasks they have supported from those they have led. If asked a question outside their expertise, they identify what needs checking. Their presentation supports the activity and communicates capability honestly.

### Contrasting case

A fictional recruitment reviewer treats a polished accent and expensive clothing as proof of technical competence. These features do not establish the relevant skill. A fair assessment needs evidence connected to the role and appropriate accessibility.

## Guided practice

**Design three presentation plans.** A: a site visit with specified protective equipment. B: an online community introduction with no dress code. C: a meeting requiring a short evidence-based recommendation. Identify functional requirements, communication preparation and one unnecessary impression-based assumption.

**Worked solution.** A: verify protective equipment and authorised access, prepare relevant questions and follow the safety procedure. B: test the accessible communication format, prepare a concise introduction and avoid treating formal attire as mandatory. C: check figures and assumptions, organise a brief recommendation and provide an accessible supporting document. Do not infer competence from visible nervousness, clothing cost or accent.

Now revise the plan for C if the participant has only preliminary data. Label uncertainty and distinguish a provisional view from a confirmed conclusion. A polished delivery cannot remove evidential limits.

Attempt before checking the model. Identify a specific correction and repeat with an equivalent fictional case. Explain why the action fits the actual context and what would change the decision.
