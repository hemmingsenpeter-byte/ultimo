# SOC-107 / L04 — Unfamiliar norms

**Draft — independent academic and subject review pending.** Outcome: Evaluate and justify decisions about unfamiliar norms in the context of etiquette and presentation.

## Orientation and retrieval

Recall SOC-102 on cultural humility. A fictional guest is invited to a gathering with unfamiliar local conventions. The invitation gives only a time and place. How can the guest prepare without assuming every person follows the same practice?

## Core teaching

Unfamiliar norms create uncertainty about what behaviour is expected or appropriate. A newcomer may not know the order of introductions, use of particular spaces, timing, dress or procedures for speaking. This uncertainty does not require memorising stereotypes or copying the first behaviour observed. The most relevant sources are the actual invitation, organiser, authorised procedure and appropriate local expertise where requested.

A useful distinction is between learning about a practice and assuming authority to interpret it for others. In Aotearoa New Zealand, tikanga may be relevant to particular Māori settings, and practices can vary across whānau, hapū, iwi and situations. Ask the appropriate host about expectations and follow their guidance where feasible and consistent with actual obligations. A generic course cannot certify cultural competence or prescribe a universal protocol. Do not require another participant to represent an entire community or answer personal questions about identity.

Mistakes can be handled through proportionate correction. If a person misunderstands the order of contributions, they can acknowledge the clarification and adapt, rather than withdrawing or making a dramatic apology. If an expectation conflicts with accessibility, safety or consent, explain the practical constraint and seek an appropriate alternative. In coercive or unsafe settings, disengagement or specialist support may be preferable to attempting conformity. Evaluate learning by accurate verification, respectful adaptation and appropriate limits, not flawless performance of unfamiliar customs.

## Framework

**Prepare–ask–observe–adapt–repair**

Review the event purpose and explicit instructions. Ask the appropriate host about relevant unfamiliar expectations. Observe without assuming a pattern is mandatory. Adapt to verified practices while preserving consent and safety. If corrected, acknowledge the specific issue and revise. Record uncertainty about local details rather than inventing universal guidance.

## Worked example

A fictional visitor attends a community gathering where they have been told there will be a formal welcome. They ask the organiser in advance whether there is an arrival time, speaking procedure or practical guidance they should know. The organiser provides local instructions and explains that guests may listen rather than speak. The visitor follows the arrangement and uses a written access question where necessary. When corrected about where to sit, they acknowledge the guidance and move to the appropriate place. They do not claim that the practice applies to every future gathering or require others to explain their identity.

### Contrasting case

A fictional newcomer observes several people perform a greeting and assumes everyone must do the same, even after someone declines physical contact. Observation alone does not establish a requirement. Respect individual choice and seek appropriate local guidance.

## Guided practice

**Respond to three fictional uncertainties.** A: no dress guidance for a formal community gathering. B: an unfamiliar speaking order. C: a practice requiring physical contact that a guest does not want. Write a question or response for each.

**Worked solution.** A: ask the organiser if there are practical requirements or guidance, without assuming a particular dress code. B: ask when contributions are invited and whether written input is welcome. C: decline the physical contact respectfully and ask about another suitable greeting if needed. Do not pressure someone to explain their refusal. Where a genuine requirement exists, verify its authority and consider an accessible alternative.

Now simulate a minor correction from the host: the guest begins speaking before the agreed time. Acknowledge the timing correction and follow the process. A prolonged self-focused apology may be unnecessary; the meaningful change is adapting conduct.

Attempt before checking the model. Identify a specific correction and repeat with an equivalent fictional case. Explain why the action fits the actual context and what would change the decision.
