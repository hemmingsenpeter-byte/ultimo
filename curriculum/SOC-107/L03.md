# SOC-107 / L03 — Hosting

**Draft — independent academic and subject review pending.** Outcome: Apply hosting in the context of etiquette and presentation.

## Orientation and retrieval

## Core teaching

Hosting involves arranging an environment in which a defined activity can take place and participants can understand the practical conditions. It includes planning, communication, access, resources, time, safety and follow-through. A host is not responsible for making every participant enjoy the event, and guests do not owe engagement or gratitude. The host's actual responsibilities depend on the role and venue; organisers should identify who controls facilities, bookings, budgets and applicable safety procedures.

Plan from the participant's journey: invitation and registration, arrival, participation, departure and information afterwards. Check the actual venue and communication format, rather than assuming access. W3C and New Zealand MSD guidance provide event accessibility planning considerations. They do not guarantee individual access or establish a venue's compliance. Ask about practical access requirements without unnecessary private details, and assign someone to verify arrangements. For food or other services, communicate relevant limitations and check applicable safety requirements rather than promising conditions that cannot be verified.

A host must also plan for change. A venue may become unavailable, attendance may exceed capacity or a participant may need a different format. Establish an authorised decision route, contingency and clear communication channel. Keep information collection proportionate, avoid publicising attendance or photos without appropriate permission, and handle serious safety concerns through relevant procedures. Evaluate the event against its purpose, access and fulfilment of commitments, not social prestige or popularity.

## Framework

**Before–arrival–during–departure–review**

Before: define purpose, budget, capacity, booking, access and communication owners. Arrival: confirm location, timing, signage and contact route. During: explain the activity, enable voluntary participation and preserve applicable safety procedures. Departure: communicate finish time and next steps. Review: record what worked, unresolved barriers and corrective actions. Verify every promise with its responsible owner.

## Worked example

A fictional community group has a budget of $300 for twenty attendees. A room costs $160, materials $60 and an optional shared refreshment provision $40, leaving $40 for contingencies. The host confirms the room capacity and asks the venue about the actual access route before advertising it. They provide an invitation with the time, cost, participation choices and organiser contact. If an access requirement cannot be confirmed, they flag it and seek a suitable alternative. A designated organiser handles changes; the room booking and budget are authorised before commitments are made. After the gathering, attendees can optionally provide feedback about logistics. The host does not publish a group photograph without appropriate permission.

### Contrasting case

A host advertises an event as fully accessible without checking the entrance, toilets, communication format or participant needs. Good intentions do not establish access. Verify specific conditions and communicate uncertainty honestly.

## Guided practice

**Audit a fictional gathering.** Budget $250; room $140, materials $50, optional refreshments $30. Capacity 18; 22 people request places. Venue access and audio arrangements are not confirmed. Identify the remaining budget, decision constraints and communication actions.

**Worked solution.** Remaining budget is $30, not evidence that every access need can be funded. Capacity is a constraint; use a transparent allocation process or arrange a verified larger venue before accepting additional attendees. Ask the venue about access and audio; identify an appropriate contact for individual requirements. Confirm bookings and costs under actual authority. Explain options to people who cannot attend rather than making unsupported promises.

Now suppose the room is cancelled on the day. The host should communicate the cancellation or verified alternative through the agreed channel, clarify any costs and follow the applicable venue arrangements. Do not send attendees to an unconfirmed replacement.

Attempt before checking the model. Identify a specific correction and repeat with an equivalent fictional case. Explain why the action fits the actual context and what would change the decision.
