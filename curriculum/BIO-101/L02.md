# BIO-101 / L02 — Circulation and respiration

**Draft — independent review pending.** Outcome: Compare and distinguish circulation and respiration in the context of human biology.

## Core teaching

Ventilation moves air into and out of the lungs. Gas exchange transfers oxygen and carbon dioxide across thin alveolar and capillary surfaces. Blood carries oxygen, largely bound to haemoglobin, to tissues. The right side of the heart pumps blood toward the lungs and the left side toward systemic tissues; blood returns through veins. Cells use oxygen in aerobic metabolism and produce carbon dioxide. This is a simplified loop: circulation also transports nutrients, hormones and heat.

## Framework

Air → alveoli → blood → heart → tissues → blood → lungs. Mark the gas exchange interfaces and direction of flow.

This sequence is an instructional model, not a universal law. Check its limitations against the contrasting case.

## Worked example

During ordinary movement, oxygen reaches muscle via inhaled air, alveolar exchange, oxygenated blood returning to the left heart, and systemic circulation. Carbon dioxide returns in blood to the lungs for exhalation.

### Contrasting case

Arteries are defined by carrying blood away from the heart, not by always carrying oxygen-rich blood; pulmonary arteries carry relatively oxygen-poor blood.

## Guided practice

Draw the route of oxygen from air to a hand muscle and back as carbon dioxide. Model: include lungs, left heart, systemic arteries, capillaries, systemic veins, right heart and pulmonary circulation.
