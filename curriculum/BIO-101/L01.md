# BIO-101 / L01 — Cells and tissues

**Draft — independent review pending.** Outcome: Explain cells and tissues in the context of human biology.

## Core teaching

Cells are living units with a membrane that regulates exchange and internal machinery that performs functions. Specialised cells form tissues; tissues combine into organs and organs into systems. Epithelial tissue lines surfaces, connective tissue supports and connects, muscle tissue contracts, and nervous tissue communicates. An organ typically contains multiple tissue types. Anatomy describes structures; physiology describes functions. The distinction helps explain why a change in tissue structure may affect an organ's performance.

## Framework

Cell → tissue → organ → system → function. Trace a function up the hierarchy rather than memorising names alone.

This sequence is an instructional model, not a universal law. Check its limitations against the contrasting case.

## Worked example

The heart contains contracting cardiac muscle, connective support, nervous signalling and epithelial linings. Its function depends on these tissues working together, not muscle alone.

### Contrasting case

A muscle cell and a skeletal muscle organ are not interchangeable terms; the organ contains other tissue and blood vessels.

## Knowledge check

1. What is a tissue?
2. What distinguishes anatomy and physiology?
3. Why can an organ contain multiple tissues?

<details><summary>Answers (open after attempting)</summary>

1. A group of cells and associated material organised for functions.
2. Structure and function respectively.
3. Different specialised functions cooperate.
