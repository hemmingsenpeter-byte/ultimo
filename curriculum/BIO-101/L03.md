## Core teaching

Digestion breaks food into substances that can be absorbed; the small intestine is a major site of absorption. The liver processes absorbed nutrients and produces bile; the pancreas supplies digestive secretions. Kidneys filter blood and selectively reabsorb or secrete substances, regulating water, electrolytes and wastes. Excretion of urine is different from elimination of undigested material in faeces. These processes rely on circulation and regulation and are not independent plumbing systems.

## Framework

Input → digestion → absorption → circulation/metabolism → regulation and waste removal. Distinguish digestive elimination from urinary excretion.

This sequence is an instructional model, not a universal law. Check its limitations against the contrasting case.

## Worked example

After eating, absorbed nutrients enter circulation through blood or lymphatic routes; kidneys continue regulating fluid and dissolved substances. The kidneys do not digest the food or remove solid dietary fibre directly.

### Contrasting case

Saying that all waste leaves through the kidneys overlooks the lungs and digestive tract.
