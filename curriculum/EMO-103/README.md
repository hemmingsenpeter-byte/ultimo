# EMO-103 — Emotional regulation

Draft · Year 2 · Level 2 guided practice · 6 indicative study hours. Not accredited. [Syllabus](syllabus.md).

## Outcomes
- Explain triggers and appraisals in the context of emotional regulation.
- Compare and distinguish alternative interpretations in the context of emotional regulation.
- Apply regulation strategies in the context of emotional regulation.
- Evaluate and justify decisions about constructive responses in the context of emotional regulation.

## Reading
- [Emotion Regulation: Current Status and Future Prospects](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/1047840X.2014.940781) — 2015, process model and consequences of regulation strategies.
- [Individual differences in two emotion regulation processes: implications for affect, relationships, and well-being](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12916575/) — 2003, abstract, reappraisal and suppression associations.
- [Regulatory Flexibility: An Individual Differences Perspective on Coping and Emotion Regulation](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26173226/) — 2013, abstract, context sensitivity, repertoire and feedback.
- [Doing What Matters in Times of Stress](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789240003927) — 2020 publication overview, skills for coping with adversity.
- [Improving Assertiveness Self-Help Resources](https://www.cci.health.wa.gov.au/Resources/Looking-After-Yourself/Assertiveness) — Workbook overview, assertiveness, requests and declining.
- [Getting support for family violence](https://www.victimsinfo.govt.nz/understanding-crime/types-of-crime/family-harm-and-abuse/family-violence/getting-support-for-family-violence) — Support options, safety planning and emergency response; checked 2026-11-26.
