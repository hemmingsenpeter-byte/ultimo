# SOC-104 / L04 — Support needs

**Draft — independent academic and qualified subject review pending.** Outcome: Evaluate and justify decisions about support needs in the context of social confidence.

## Orientation and retrieval

Retrieve the confidence-skill matrix and MEN-106's support routes. A fictional learner struggles to participate in an ordinary group activity. How can you distinguish an access need, a skill gap, a preference and a concern requiring qualified support?

## Core teaching

Social participation varies with personal preferences, roles, access, experience and health. Some people prefer limited interaction or written communication and have no difficulty with their chosen arrangements. Others want to participate but face a structural barrier, such as inaccessible scheduling, language, unclear expectations or exclusion. Still others may need specific instruction or qualified support. A single observation of quietness, hesitation or nervousness cannot distinguish these possibilities.

Social anxiety disorder is a clinical condition involving significant fear of social scrutiny or evaluation and interference with functioning; the NIMH public guidance distinguishes it from ordinary shyness and describes treatment options. A qualified professional assesses whether a condition is present. This curriculum does not diagnose, provide clinical exposure plans, recommend medication changes or tell learners to endure distress. People can seek professional support if they want or if persistent difficulties affect ordinary functioning; they need not prove a threshold to a peer or tutor.

Match assistance to the communicated need. An access issue may require an authorised accommodation; an unclear procedure may require explanation; a specific skill gap may benefit from voluntary practice; a clinical concern may require a qualified provider. A person who wants privacy may not want an intervention at all. Use local emergency assistance for immediate danger. A peer can offer practical help or information within their role, but cannot guarantee a treatment outcome or confidentiality beyond actual obligations. The objective is appropriate routing rather than maximising sociability.

## Framework

**Preference–barrier–skill–support map**

Identify the fictional person's stated goal, if any. Separate chosen preference from a constraint preventing desired participation. Identify task evidence and missing instructions or access needs. Offer an appropriate educational, organisational or qualified support route with consent. For immediate danger use emergency assistance. Review only the relevant decision; do not classify the individual.

Text model: expressed need → match type of support → check access and authority → appropriate route.

## Worked example

A fictional learner prefers contributing to meetings in writing and completes all agreed work. No intervention is necessarily needed. A second wants to contribute but cannot access the meeting platform; the appropriate route is accessible technology or an authorised alternative. A third reports persistent fear and difficulty fulfilling ordinary responsibilities and asks for help; offer information about a qualified health professional. These are different support needs. The same visible behaviour, limited speaking, does not determine which case applies.

### Contrasting case

A group leader insists every quiet participant must complete a public-speaking challenge to prove confidence. This conflates preference, access and health with a compulsory performance. It may exceed the leader's role and cannot establish clinical improvement.

## Guided practice

**Match fictional cases to routes.** A: a person prefers occasional written communication. B: a student wants to join but the venue is inaccessible. C: a learner lacks the instructions for introductions. D: someone reports persistent impairment and requests help. E: immediate danger.

**Worked solution.** A: respect preference; no change required. B: contact the responsible organiser or accessibility service, with consent and a feasible alternative. C: provide relevant teaching and optional practice. D: help identify qualified care; no diagnosis or treatment advice. E: use local emergency services promptly. Multiple needs may coexist; a learner can choose a written format while also accessing other support.

Now identify one appropriate question for B: 'What access information or alternative format would help you participate?' Avoid requesting clinical details unnecessary for the accommodation.

Attempt before checking the worked solution. Identify a specific reasoning or communication correction and repeat using an equivalent fictional case. Assess observable task quality, not personality, popularity or another person's compliance.
