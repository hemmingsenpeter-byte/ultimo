## Core teaching

Feedback is information about performance relative to a goal or criterion. Useful feedback distinguishes the desired outcome, what occurred and the next step. Hattie and Timperley's educational review examines feedback's different functions and levels. Effects depend on design and context; feedback is not automatically beneficial. A vague personality judgement provides little information about how to improve, while a specific observation can support a testable correction.

Distinguish observation from interpretation. 'The event time was omitted' describes a verifiable gap. 'You are not a people person' is a broad personal verdict unsupported by the task. Feedback may concern task accuracy, communication process or how the learner checks their own work. A learner can ask for evidence, clarify the criterion and disagree with an interpretation. The observer can also be mistaken. Do not evaluate eye contact, voice, nervousness or emotional disclosure unless a relevant objective justifies the criterion and accessibility has been addressed.

An improvement cycle should preserve what worked, identify one consequential gap and test a specific change on an equivalent task. Avoid overwhelming learners with numerous stylistic demands or treating someone else's compliance as the performance metric. Feedback must be voluntary and respectful in ordinary practice; formal assessments may have defined requirements but should remain fair and accessible. The goal is usable evidence about the skill, not approval of the person.

## Framework

**Goal–observation–gap–next attempt**

State the agreed task criterion. Record an observable strength and gap, separating interpretation from fact. Invite the learner's correction or context. Choose one actionable improvement, identify a comparable new task and review against the same criterion. Preserve privacy and accessibility. If the feedback is unsupported or hostile, seek appropriate clarification or another suitable reviewer.

Text model: target → observed performance → specific gap → revised action → retest.

## Worked example

A fictional learner describes an event to a peer. The observer says: 'You gave the purpose and date accurately, but you didn't mention the cost. Next time include the confirmed cost or state that it's unknown.' The learner explains that the price was missing from the brief. The observer updates the feedback: rather than invent a price, the learner should flag the missing information and identify the organiser's contact. A new practice case tests whether they do so. This feedback is more useful than telling the learner to act confident, and it incorporates the learner's correction.

### Contrasting case

An observer says 'You are awkward and should make more eye contact.' The statement does not specify a relevant task gap and may reflect an unsupported style preference. Ask which objective was not met, or use another appropriate reviewer. Do not make personal disclosure or conformity a condition of receiving feedback.

## Guided practice

**Critique three fictional feedback statements.** A: 'You forgot to confirm the meeting date.' B: 'You're bad at socialising.' C: 'The recipient said the instructions were unclear; let's identify which detail was missing.' Rewrite where necessary and specify a new practice task.

**Worked solution.** A identifies an observable gap; add a next step such as confirming the date before ending the exchange. B is a global verdict; replace with actual evidence if available, or reject the unsupported claim. C invites investigation and a specific correction; check the recipient's actual concern before concluding. A comparable task might use a different event with the same information requirements.

Now suppose the learner disagrees with A because the date was confirmed in writing. Verify that evidence and revise the feedback. Correcting the observer is part of a fair learning process.

Attempt before checking the worked solution. Identify a specific reasoning or communication correction and repeat using an equivalent fictional case. Assess observable task quality, not personality, popularity or another person's compliance.
