# MEN-103 / L03 — If-then plans

**Draft — independent academic and qualified subject review pending.** Outcome: Apply if-then plans in the context of motivation and procrastination.

## Orientation and retrieval

Retrieve the next feasible action from L02. A learner intends to practise a language every week but repeatedly forgets to start. How could a reliable cue be linked to a specific response?

## Core teaching

A goal intention describes what a person wants to achieve. An implementation intention adds a conditional plan linking a recognisable situation to a chosen response: if situation X occurs, then I will do Y. The distinction is important because knowing what matters does not necessarily determine the moment or manner of action. The cue should be identifiable and reasonably likely to occur; the response should be feasible, appropriate and relevant to the goal.

Research reviews report benefits from implementation intentions across varied goal contexts. These results are averages, not guarantees, and the strategy cannot supply missing skills, resources or willingness. A plan can fail when the cue is vague, the action is too demanding, several plans conflict or the environment is unpredictable. The response should not override new information, safety or another person's consent. In conditions that vary significantly, a fallback or rescheduling rule may be better than an inflexible routine.

Distinguish initiation plans from coping plans. An initiation plan specifies when to start a task. A coping plan anticipates an ordinary obstacle and selects a suitable alternative. For example, a planned session interrupted by essential care duties might move to an available window, rather than creating a penalty for missed practice. An if–then plan is successful when it helps complete a meaningful objective sustainably. It is not a measure of willpower or a clinical intervention.

## Framework

**Cue–response–fallback–test**

Choose a goal that remains useful. Specify an observable cue (event, location or agreed time) and a small response. Add a fallback for a plausible barrier, plus a cancellation rule for safety or essential duties. Test on several low-stakes opportunities and record cue occurrence, response and reason for any miss. Change one variable if the plan repeatedly fails.

Template: If [specific feasible situation], then [specific action], unless [legitimate exception]; if [ordinary obstacle], then [safe fallback].

## Worked example

A fictional learner wants to review logic twice weekly. Their original plan is 'when I feel motivated, study'. They replace it with 'after putting the evening dishes away on Tuesday and Thursday, open the prepared question sheet and answer one question before checking the key, provided no essential duty needs attention'. If the evening is unexpectedly occupied, the fallback is to choose an available window within the next two days. The first week, Tuesday works but Thursday's cue never occurs because the learner eats away from home. The learner does not interpret this as a personal failure; they select a cue that occurs more reliably.

### Contrasting case

A plan saying 'if I feel tired, I will force myself to continue' is not a safe implementation intention. It ignores the meaning of fatigue and may conflict with legitimate recovery or safety needs.

## Guided practice

### Repair three if–then plans

1. 'If I have time, work harder.' 2. 'Every time any message appears, reply immediately.' 3. 'After the 9:00 meeting, write the first paragraph of the report' — but the meeting sometimes runs into urgent work.

### Worked solution

1. Define an actual cue and action: after opening the prepared document at an agreed session, write one evidence-backed bullet. 2. Match the response to urgency; capture deferrable items and preserve essential channels. 3. Retain the specific cue but add a fallback: if an urgent duty intervenes, record the next action and reschedule within the agreed deadline. Feedback: a valid plan connects a recognisable event to a feasible response and respects exceptions.

Attempt the task before checking the worked solution. Identify one justified correction, then repeat using an equivalent fictional case. The criterion is quality of reasoning, not personal performance or symptom change.

## Independent transfer
