**Draft — independent academic and qualified subject review pending.** Outcome: Evaluate and justify decisions about barrier review in the context of motivation and procrastination.

## Core teaching

A barrier review examines why an intended behaviour did not occur and what could change the conditions. Begin with observable facts, not a judgement about character. Separate goal relevance, opportunity, resources, capability, task design, competing priorities, emotional discomfort and health-related constraints. These categories may overlap; they are not diagnostic criteria. An unmet plan can provide useful information about the plan itself.

A simple self-directed comparison can test a practical change, but it is not a controlled clinical trial. Measure the opportunity to begin, actual initiation, completed output and perceived feasibility separately. If the cue did not occur, the denominator of opportunities matters. If a learner starts but cannot finish, the issue may be task difficulty or dependencies rather than initiation. If the goal no longer makes sense, abandon or revise it. Avoid treating a streak or percentage as an indicator of worth.

Persistent difficulties causing significant distress or interference with everyday responsibilities can have many explanations and may merit professional assessment. Psychological-treatment research does not authorise a student or tutor to diagnose or provide therapy. Support may also include accommodation, workload changes or help from a teacher. Do not design experiments involving sleep deprivation, punitive consequences, medication changes or deliberate distress. The aim is to learn which manageable conditions support a valuable action.

## Framework

**Review by opportunity**

Record: planned cue; cue occurred (yes/no); response initiated (yes/no/not applicable); usable output; obstacle; appropriate next change. After a small set of opportunities, identify the common obstacle and test one modification. Retain necessary support and stop if the experiment becomes burdensome. Compare outcomes using the same task-quality criterion. This is a teaching worksheet, not a treatment protocol.

## Worked example

A fictional learner schedules five practice opportunities. The cue occurs four times, and they start twice. In one missed opportunity they lack the reading; in another they have an essential appointment. The fifth cue never occurs. Of the two starts, one produces a correct answer and one reveals a misunderstanding. A simplistic report of 2/5 would conceal several distinct issues. A useful review separates initiation conditional on cue occurrence (2/4) from completion quality and availability. The learner prepares materials and revises the schedule; they ask a tutor to clarify the misunderstood concept.

### Contrasting case

A learner whose schedule is overloaded by paid work and caring responsibilities is not necessarily avoiding a task. Reducing the goal or seeking structural support may be more appropriate than tracking every missed session.

## Guided practice

### Analyse a five-opportunity synthetic record

A: cue yes, action yes, correct output; B: cue yes, action no, missing materials; C: cue no, action not applicable; D: cue yes, action yes, incorrect output; E: cue yes, action no, essential duty. Compute the observed initiation rate conditional on cue occurrence; identify barriers and recommend a next test.

### Worked solution

Four cues occurred; two actions began: 50% initiation among cue occurrences. This is descriptive, not a stable estimate of ability. One miss relates to materials, one to a legitimate duty, and one cue never occurred. One incorrect output needs feedback. A feasible next test prepares materials in advance and retains the safety/duty exception. Because several factors differ, do not claim a causal effect from these five observations.

Attempt the task before checking the worked solution. Identify one justified correction, then repeat using an equivalent fictional case. The criterion is quality of reasoning, not personal performance or symptom change.
