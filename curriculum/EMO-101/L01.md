# EMO-101 / L01 — Emotion labels

**Draft — independent review pending.** Outcome: Explain emotion labels in the context of emotional identification.

## Orientation and retrieval

Before reading, attempt the first knowledge check without notes. Write a baseline explanation and mark uncertainties.

## Core teaching

Emotions involve experience, appraisals, bodily responses and action tendencies, and people differ in how they label them. A racing heart can occur with excitement, fear, exertion or other causes; it is not proof of a particular emotion. A label is a tentative description, not a diagnosis. Research on affect labelling has investigated responses to emotional stimuli, but laboratory findings do not guarantee that naming an emotion will resolve distress. Practise descriptive vocabulary and allow uncertainty.

## Framework

Notice sensation → propose a tentative feeling label → check context → revise. Use 'I might be feeling…' rather than diagnosing.

This sequence is an instructional model, not a universal law. Check its limitations against the contrasting case.

## Worked example

A fictional employee receives unexpected criticism and notices a tight chest. They consider embarrassment, anxiety and frustration. They cannot determine the exact emotion from the sensation alone, and they ask for clarification.

### Contrasting case

A colleague looks quiet; calling them depressed based on appearance confuses observation with diagnosis.

## Guided practice

For a fictional delayed message, propose three plausible emotions and one neutral observation. Model: worry, disappointment or irritation; observation: no reply has arrived by the agreed time.

Attempt the exercise before comparing to the model answer. Correct one specific gap.

## Independent transfer

Write a fictional emotion vocabulary map for a stressful and an enjoyable event, including uncertainty.
