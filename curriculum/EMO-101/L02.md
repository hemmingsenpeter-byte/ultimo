# EMO-101 / L02 — Event and interpretation

**Draft — independent review pending.** Outcome: Compare and distinguish event and interpretation in the context of emotional identification.

## Orientation and retrieval

Before reading, attempt the first knowledge check without notes. Recall Emotion labels and connect it to this topic.

## Core teaching

Events, interpretations, feelings and actions are analytically distinct even though they can influence each other rapidly. An event is observable information; interpretation is the meaning assigned; a feeling is subjective experience; an action is behaviour. Appraisals can vary between people and across contexts. This educational distinction is not a claim that every emotion is preceded by a conscious thought. Recognising alternative interpretations can prevent premature certainty; it must never be used to dismiss credible danger.

## Framework

Event → possible interpretation → feeling → chosen action. Draw return arrows because actions change subsequent events.

This sequence is an instructional model, not a universal law. Check its limitations against the contrasting case.

## Worked example

A manager says 'Please revise this.' Event: the request. Interpretation A: the work needs changes. Interpretation B: the manager thinks I am incompetent. Feelings and response may differ. The evidence supports the request, not necessarily either broader interpretation.

### Contrasting case

If someone makes a credible threat, searching for a benign interpretation must not replace safety measures.

## Guided practice

Separate the four components in a fictional cancelled meeting. Model: event: meeting cancelled; interpretation: perhaps workload changed; feeling: disappointment; action: ask whether to reschedule.

Attempt the exercise before comparing to the model answer. Correct one specific gap.

## Independent transfer

Map an unfamiliar scenario involving good news and identify at least one alternative interpretation.

## Knowledge check

1. Which part is directly observable?
2. Is a feeling evidence that an interpretation is true?
3. Why include a return arrow?
