# EMO-101 / L04 — Response choices

**Draft — independent review pending.** Outcome: Evaluate and justify decisions about response choices in the context of emotional identification.

## Orientation and retrieval

Before reading, attempt the first knowledge check without notes. Recall Needs and connect it to this topic.

## Core teaching

A response can acknowledge an emotion while choosing behaviour consistent with values and context. Regulation does not mean suppressing every feeling or always staying calm. In ordinary disagreements, options include asking a clarifying question, expressing a concern, delaying a response with an agreed return time, or seeking support. In unsafe situations, prioritise distance and help rather than conflict skills. The appropriate option depends on urgency, power and safety.

## Framework

Notice → assess safety → identify goal → choose response → review impact. Use a fictional scenario if personal discussion feels uncomfortable.

This sequence is an instructional model, not a universal law. Check its limitations against the contrasting case.

## Worked example

A colleague criticises a draft publicly. The learner says, 'I want to understand the revision. Could we discuss specifics after this meeting?' They can later request feedback privately. This separates immediate emotion from a constructive action.

### Contrasting case

Remaining in a threatening conversation to prove emotional control can increase risk; leaving may be appropriate.

## Guided practice

Compare two responses to a fictional harsh email: an immediate insult versus asking for examples after a break. Model: the latter may clarify the task and reduce escalation, but the learner can set boundaries about disrespect.

Attempt the exercise before comparing to the model answer. Correct one specific gap.

## Independent transfer

Write two possible responses to an unfamiliar situation, justify each, and identify a safety or referral boundary.
