# ART-101 / L01 — Observation and negative space

**Draft — independent review pending.** Outcome: Explain observation and negative space in the context of drawing and observation.

## Orientation and retrieval

Before reading, attempt the first knowledge check without notes. Write a baseline explanation and mark uncertainties.

## Core teaching

Observation in drawing means looking at actual relationships rather than relying on a symbolic idea of an object. Positive space contains the subject; negative space surrounds or sits between its parts. Both contribute to the composition. Drawing a gap can reveal alignment errors because the gap has a shape of its own. Contours describe boundaries, not every interior detail. These are drawing methods, not claims about artistic worth or a preferred style.

## Framework

Observe whole → mark major shapes → inspect gaps → compare edges → revise. Use light marks first.

This sequence is an instructional model, not a universal law. Check its limitations against the contrasting case.

## Worked example

A mug handle looks like a curved symbol in memory. Observing the enclosed gap reveals a narrow oval tilted sideways. Drawing that gap first helps locate the handle relative to the mug body.

### Contrasting case

A deliberately stylised logo may simplify negative space for expressive reasons; observational accuracy is not the only artistic aim.

## Guided practice

Draw an ordinary object or describe its outline using a visual reference. Model: identify outer shape and two negative-space shapes, compare widths and revise one edge.

Attempt the exercise before comparing to the model answer. Correct one specific gap.

## Independent transfer

Make a second study of a chair or plant, focusing on gaps between parts rather than details.
