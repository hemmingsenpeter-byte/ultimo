## Core teaching

Art critique compares a work with its stated intention using observable features. Describe before interpreting: line, shape, proportion, value and space are evidence you can discuss. Then explain how those features contribute to the intended effect and propose a specific revision. A critique should distinguish craft objectives from subjective preference, cultural context and accessibility. Improvement is not necessarily making every drawing realistic.

## Framework

Describe → interpret → compare with intention → propose change → test → reflect.

This sequence is an instructional model, not a universal law. Check its limitations against the contrasting case.

## Worked example

A learner intended a quiet still life, but several high-contrast edges compete. The critique identifies those edges, suggests reducing one contrast, and compares a second thumbnail. Another viewer may prefer the energetic original.

### Contrasting case

'This is bad art' provides neither evidence nor an actionable revision and may confuse taste with craft.

## Guided practice

Review two versions of a fictional drawing. Model: state the intention, name two observable changes, explain the likely effect and choose a revision.

Attempt the exercise before comparing to the model answer. Correct one specific gap.
