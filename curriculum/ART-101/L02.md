# ART-101 / L02 — Proportion

**Draft — independent review pending.** Outcome: Compare and distinguish proportion in the context of drawing and observation.

## Orientation and retrieval

Before reading, attempt the first knowledge check without notes. Recall Observation and negative space and connect it to this topic.

## Core teaching

Proportion is the size relationship between parts. Use comparative measurement: choose a reference unit, estimate other lengths relative to it, and check alignments. Perspective describes how spatial relationships appear from a viewpoint; distant equal-sized objects may appear smaller, and parallel lines receding in certain directions can appear to converge. Not every image follows linear perspective, and deliberate distortion may serve an artistic purpose.

## Framework

Choose reference → compare ratios → mark landmarks → check alignment → revise. Keep viewpoint consistent.

This sequence is an instructional model, not a universal law. Check its limitations against the contrasting case.

## Worked example

In a still life, a bottle appears twice the height of a nearby cup. Mark the cup as one unit and bottle as two; compare the horizontal spacing. If the drawing shows the bottle three units high, revise the relative length.

### Contrasting case

Measuring the real bottle height with a ruler does not directly give apparent size from a particular viewpoint when distances differ.

## Guided practice

Draw two simple rectangles with an observed 2:3 height ratio. Model: if first is 4 cm, second is 6 cm. Then adjust an intentionally incorrect 4:8 sketch.

Attempt the exercise before comparing to the model answer. Correct one specific gap.

## Independent transfer

Create a simple still-life study, annotate three relative measurements and explain one perspective choice.
