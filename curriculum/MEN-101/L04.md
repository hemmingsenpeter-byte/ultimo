# MEN-101 / L04 — Iteration

**Draft — independent review pending.** Outcome: Evaluate and justify decisions about iteration in the context of habits.

## Orientation and retrieval

Before reading, attempt the first knowledge check without notes. Recall Implementation intentions and connect it to this topic.

## Core teaching

Behaviour tracking is a measurement tool, not a verdict on character. Track opportunities and attempts separately: a missed action when its cue never occurred differs from a missed action despite the cue. Review a small sample cautiously. Self-reported automaticity and adherence are distinct. A seven-day trial can identify barriers, but cannot establish a durable habit or cause-and-effect. Adjust the design rather than punish missed days.

## Framework

Log cue/attempt/barrier → calculate attempt proportion → identify pattern → revise one change → retest.

This sequence is an instructional model, not a universal law. Check its limitations against the contrasting case.

## Worked example

A learner has five cue opportunities in seven days and acts four times. Adherence conditional on the cue is 4/5, not 4/7. Two days without the cue suggest the plan needs a backup.

### Contrasting case

A perfect seven-day streak is not proof of a permanent habit and does not justify escalating the workload.

## Guided practice

Create a seven-row fictional log with cue, attempt and barrier. Model: when four of six opportunities lead to action, report 4/6 and identify what differed on missed days.

Attempt the exercise before comparing to the model answer. Correct one specific gap.

## Independent transfer

Run the seven-day capstone with a private or fictional log, revise one variable, and compare with a second trial.

## Knowledge check

1. What distinguishes adherence from automaticity?
2. Why count cue opportunities?
3. What can seven days establish?

<details><summary>Answers (open after attempting)</summary>

1. Doing the action versus how automatically it occurs.
2. To distinguish plan failure from missing contexts.
3. Early feasibility and barriers, not durable mastery.

</details>
