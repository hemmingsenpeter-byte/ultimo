# MEN-101 / L02 — Environmental friction

**Draft — independent review pending.** Outcome: Compare and distinguish environmental friction in the context of habits.

## Orientation and retrieval

Before reading, attempt the first knowledge check without notes. Recall Cues and routines and connect it to this topic.

## Core teaching

Environmental friction is a practical design heuristic: effort, time and interruptions can make a behaviour less convenient. Changing the surroundings may make a desired action easier to start. It does not remove structural constraints such as long work hours or caring duties. Avoid equating convenience with moral virtue; the goal is to reduce unnecessary barriers and preserve choice. A change should be reversible and safe.

## Framework

Identify barrier → remove one step → make cue visible → test → compare. Change one variable when possible.

This sequence is an instructional model, not a universal law. Check its limitations against the contrasting case.

## Worked example

A learner wants to practise drawing but supplies are in a distant cupboard. Putting paper and a pencil beside the desk reduces preparation. If time is the actual barrier, the change may do little.

### Contrasting case

Buying a more expensive notebook does not solve a lack of time or an unclear task.

## Guided practice

Sketch a fictional study environment and identify three steps before the first action. Model: open the relevant page before ending yesterday's session; store distracting notifications out of view during a chosen interval.

Attempt the exercise before comparing to the model answer. Correct one specific gap.

## Independent transfer

Redesign a fictional kitchen routine without removing someone else's access or creating a hazard.
