## Core teaching

An implementation intention links a defined situation to an intended action, often expressed as if-then. It differs from a goal intention: 'I want to read more' describes a desired outcome; 'if I finish lunch, I read one page' identifies an opportunity and action. Research supports implementation intentions in many settings, but they do not guarantee success and can be poorly suited to unpredictable situations. Include a realistic fallback that maintains autonomy.

## Framework

Goal → likely cue → concrete action → fallback → record. Keep the action small enough for ordinary circumstances.

This sequence is an instructional model, not a universal law. Check its limitations against the contrasting case.

## Worked example

If I sit at my desk after breakfast, I will write three lines for my course. If my morning is interrupted, I will use the first quiet ten-minute interval later. This is more specific than promising to study whenever motivated.

### Contrasting case

If I feel inspired, I will study for four hours is vague and difficult to test.

## Guided practice

Write an if-then plan for a short reading task. Model: if I return from lunch and have five minutes, open the lesson and answer one question; otherwise choose the next free interval.

Attempt the exercise before comparing to the model answer. Correct one specific gap.

## Independent transfer
