## Core teaching

Habits are learned tendencies for behaviour to occur in recurring contexts; not every repeated action is automatic. Context cues can become associated with a response over time. Lally and colleagues studied repeated everyday behaviours and found variability in automaticity trajectories; no fixed number of days guarantees a habit. A cue should be observable, frequent enough and connected to an action that is feasible. Motivation may help initiate a routine but is not a substitute for a workable environment.

## Framework

Cue → behaviour → immediate consequence → repeat → review context. Define the action in observable terms.

This sequence is an instructional model, not a universal law. Check its limitations against the contrasting case.

## Worked example

After making tea, Ari opens a notebook and writes one sentence. The tea preparation is a cue; writing is the behaviour. Whether it becomes automatic is uncertain and depends on repeated context and other factors.

### Contrasting case

'Be disciplined tomorrow' provides neither a cue nor a specified action.

## Guided practice

Choose a harmless small behaviour and name a recurring cue. Model: after clearing dinner dishes, place tomorrow's book on the table. Track whether the cue occurs and whether the action follows.

Attempt the exercise before comparing to the model answer. Correct one specific gap.

## Independent transfer
