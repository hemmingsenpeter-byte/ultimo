# MEN-106 / L02 — Demands and resources

**Draft — independent academic and qualified subject review pending.** Outcome: Compare and distinguish demands and resources in the context of stress management.

## Orientation and retrieval

Retrieve the stressor–response distinction. A fictional team has 35 hours of assigned work and 25 available hours. Is a personal coping technique sufficient to resolve the discrepancy?

## Core teaching

Demands are requirements or pressures that consume time, attention, effort or other resources. Resources include information, skill, staffing, autonomy, predictable schedules, accessible tools, social support and recovery opportunity. A demand-resource audit asks whether a goal is feasible given its conditions. Some demands are meaningful and chosen; others are imposed, conflicting or unsafe. Resource adequacy depends on the particular task and individual circumstances.

Occupational health guidance treats psychosocial risks as matters of work design and environment, not solely individual resilience. Unclear roles, excessive workloads, limited control or inadequate support can require organisational intervention. A learner should not be expected to compensate indefinitely for systemic problems through time management or emotional regulation. Distinguish what can be changed personally, what requires another person's cooperation or authority, and what must be referred to a qualified or responsible organisation.

Measurement can help make the mismatch visible, but estimates are uncertain. Adding planned hours is not sufficient if tasks require incompatible deadlines, scarce skills or recovery. Do not infer that a worker can perform at maximum capacity continuously. A review may need to change the goal, increase resources, alter priorities or remove unnecessary demands. Negotiation should preserve essential safety requirements and avoid merely transferring excessive pressure to someone else.

## Framework

### Demand–resource–authority matrix

For each fictional demand, record required output, deadline, approximate resource needs, available capacity, consequence of delay and who can authorise change. Mark constraints that cannot be overridden. Categorise possible actions: remove/modify demand; increase or improve resources; clarify priorities; escalate. Agree a review point. The matrix is original planning instruction, not a workplace risk assessment or legal opinion.

## Worked example

A fictional project assistant receives three tasks: an essential check requiring six hours, a report requiring ten, and a presentation requiring eight. Only sixteen working hours are available. The total estimated demand is 24 hours, leaving an eight-hour gap before allowing for uncertainty. An appropriate response is to protect the essential check, discuss report scope and negotiate the presentation deadline or additional support with the responsible manager. Asking the assistant to 'focus harder' does not resolve the arithmetic. Reassess once actual priorities and time estimates are confirmed.

### Contrasting case

If a task is demanding because the learner lacks a skill, training or supervision may be the right resource. Simply extending deadlines might not resolve the problem. Conversely, training cannot make a structurally impossible workload feasible.

## Guided practice

### Audit a synthetic workload

A fictional volunteer coordinator has 12 available hours. Required tasks: safety administration 4 hours, event planning 6, newsletter 4, optional redesign 3. Build the matrix. Calculate the estimated gap and propose two options.

### Worked solution

Estimated demand 17 hours versus 12 available: gap 5. Protect the essential administration. Defer the optional redesign (3 hours), then negotiate at least two hours of newsletter scope or obtain appropriate assistance. An alternative is an authorised redistribution of work that does not create excessive demands elsewhere. Note that time estimates and essential requirements need confirmation. Do not propose skipping a safety duty.

Attempt the task before checking the worked solution. Identify one justified correction, then repeat using an equivalent fictional case. The criterion is quality of reasoning, not personal performance or symptom change.

## Independent transfer

Create a demand-resource matrix for a novel fictional study, household or workplace scenario. Include at least one non-negotiable duty, one missing resource and one action requiring another person's agreement. Compare alternatives and name a review trigger.
