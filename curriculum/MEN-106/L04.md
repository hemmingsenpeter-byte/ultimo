## Core teaching

Escalation means involving additional support when a difficulty exceeds the resources or authority available to manage it safely. It can include contacting an educator, manager, accessibility service, trusted person, primary-care provider or specialist service. Escalation is not confined to emergencies. The earlier a structural barrier or persistent difficulty is identified, the more options may be available. A peer or AI tutor is not a clinician and should not diagnose, prescribe or promise confidentiality beyond their actual role.

Use the urgency and nature of the problem to select the support route. A workload or access problem may need organisational action. Persistent distress or interference with sleep, work, study or relationships may warrant a health professional. If a person is in immediate danger or there is a medical emergency, contact local emergency services promptly. In New Zealand, Health New Zealand identifies 111 for immediate danger and free call or text 1737 for conversation with trained counsellors. 1737 is a support service, not a substitute for emergency response. Other countries need local alternatives. Contact information must be rechecked before publishing or relying on it.

An appropriate supportive conversation uses observable concerns, an offer of help and respect for autonomy, without interrogation. Ask whether the person would like assistance finding a suitable service. Avoid promises to solve the issue, demands to disclose personal history, or unsolicited treatment instructions. Where safety or safeguarding requires action, follow the applicable procedures and seek qualified guidance. Keep the course portfolio fictional; do not upload identifiable health information.

## Framework

**Recognise–route–support–review**

Recognise the concern and whether immediate danger is present; route to the appropriate emergency, professional, organisational or ordinary support; offer practical assistance within your role; review agreed next steps without taking responsibility for another person's treatment. Immediate danger overrides ordinary scheduling. This is a literacy and referral framework, not triage certification or therapy.

## Worked example

A fictional colleague says they cannot keep up with recurring demands and their usual functioning is affected. A peer might respond: 'I've noticed the deadlines have been difficult, and you mentioned it's affecting everyday life. Would it help to speak with the person responsible for workload or a health professional? I can help find the contact details if you want.' They do not ask for a diagnosis or recommend treatment. If the person indicates immediate danger, the peer follows emergency procedures and contacts appropriate services rather than waiting for a routine appointment.

### Contrasting case

If someone is merely frustrated by a minor, resolved problem and does not request help, repeatedly insisting that they seek treatment may be intrusive. Offer appropriate support without making assumptions. Conversely, immediate danger should never be dismissed as ordinary stress.

## Guided practice

### Match five fictional cases to support

A: unclear project priorities; B: inaccessible learning material; C: persistent distress affecting usual functioning; D: immediate danger; E: a friend wants ordinary emotional support after a difficult day. Identify an appropriate route and a role boundary for each.

### Worked solution

A: responsible manager or educator; do not decide priorities without authority. B: accessibility service or course provider; do not demand private medical information. C: encourage professional care and offer help accessing it; no diagnosis. D: local emergency services; in New Zealand call 111 for immediate danger. E: listen if invited and respect consent; no need to medicalise an ordinary experience. A person may need more than one route. Use a fictional scenario rather than recording real health information.

Attempt the task before checking the worked solution. Identify one justified correction, then repeat using an equivalent fictional case. The criterion is quality of reasoning, not personal performance or symptom change.
