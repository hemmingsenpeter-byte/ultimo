# SOC-101 / L02 — Turn-taking

## Core teaching

Turn-taking allows participants to contribute and respond. Signals such as pauses, phrasing and direct invitations can help, but vary across languages, cultures and individuals. Some overlaps express enthusiasm rather than rudeness; some silences allow thinking. Do not infer interest from eye contact or speed alone. A useful aim is mutual intelligibility and room to participate, not equal seconds of speech.

## Framework

Offer a contribution → pause → invite → listen → check. Adjust when someone is repeatedly interrupted or unable to finish.

This sequence is an instructional model, not a universal law. Check its limitations against the contrasting case.

## Worked example

During lunch, one person tells a long story. A listener waits for a natural pause, asks a relevant question and later contributes a related experience. If another participant has not spoken, an optional invitation creates room.

### Contrasting case

Rigidly timing everyone for exactly 30 seconds may undermine a natural conversation and accessibility.

## Guided practice

Write a dialogue with three turns per person. Model: each response connects to the preceding turn, includes a pause, and allows the other person to decline.

Attempt the exercise before comparing to the model answer. Correct one specific gap.

## Independent transfer

Review a fictional group exchange and identify two opportunities to make participation easier without forcing anyone to speak.

## Knowledge check

1. Does silence necessarily indicate disinterest?
2. What matters more than equal seconds?
3. How can you repair interruption?

<details><summary>Answers (open after attempting)</summary>

1. No.
2. Meaningful room to participate.
3. Acknowledge and invite the person to finish.
