# SOC-101 / L03 — Conversational repair

## Core teaching

Conversational repair addresses a misunderstanding, interruption or awkward moment. It often begins by acknowledging uncertainty or your own contribution rather than defending an assumed interpretation. Clarifying questions can distinguish meaning from intent. If harm occurred, acknowledge it specifically and change behaviour; an apology does not demand forgiveness. Ordinary conversational repair is not a substitute for safety in threatening situations.

## Framework

Notice mismatch → acknowledge → clarify → adjust → check. Keep repair proportionate to the issue.

This sequence is an instructional model, not a universal law. Check its limitations against the contrasting case.

## Worked example

You misunderstand a colleague's reference to a deadline and interrupt. Say 'Sorry, I thought you meant Friday. Did you mean next Wednesday?' They correct the date, and the conversation continues.

### Contrasting case

Explaining repeatedly why you interrupted without letting them speak can prolong the problem.

## Guided practice

Rewrite a defensive response to a fictional misunderstood joke. Model: 'I can see that landed differently than I intended. Sorry. I'll avoid that joke.' Do not insist the listener accept it.

Attempt the exercise before comparing to the model answer. Correct one specific gap.

## Independent transfer

Create a fictional dialogue where a misunderstood phrase is corrected and both people can move on or end the exchange.
