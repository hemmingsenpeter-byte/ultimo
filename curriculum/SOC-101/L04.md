# SOC-101 / L04 — Closing respectfully

**Draft — independent review pending.** Outcome: Evaluate and justify decisions about closing respectfully in the context of conversation.

## Orientation and retrieval

Before reading, attempt the first knowledge check without notes. Recall Conversational repair and connect it to this topic.

## Core teaching

A respectful close recognises that conversations have constraints. Signal the transition clearly, acknowledge the exchange, and if appropriate offer a low-pressure next step. If the other person seems ready to leave, do not block or prolong the exchange. A future invitation should be optional; a polite goodbye is sufficient. Follow-up contact requires permission and context, especially when personal details are involved.

## Framework

Acknowledge → state transition → optional invitation → accept response → leave.

This sequence is an instructional model, not a universal law. Check its limitations against the contrasting case.

## Worked example

At a networking event: 'I enjoyed hearing about your project. I'm going to say hello to the host. Would you be open to exchanging emails?' If the answer is no or uncertain, thank them and move on.

### Contrasting case

Demanding contact details or interpreting politeness as romantic interest disregards autonomy.

## Guided practice

Write three closings: a casual chat, a work meeting and a conversation you wish to end quickly. Model: each includes a clear transition and no obligation to continue.

Attempt the exercise before comparing to the model answer. Correct one specific gap.

## Independent transfer

Role-play a five-minute conversation with an optional future invitation and a gracious response to refusal.
