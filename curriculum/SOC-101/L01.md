# SOC-101 / L01 — Open questions

## Core teaching

Open questions allow another person to choose how much detail to provide. Follow-up questions connect to what the person just said; they differ from a checklist of unrelated topics. Research has linked question-asking, particularly follow-ups, with interpersonal liking in studied conversations, but liking is not a guaranteed outcome and the original paper has a correction. The goal here is mutual understanding, not manipulating approval. Offer your own relevant contributions and respect unwillingness to answer.

## Framework

Invite → listen → follow up on one detail → share something relevant → allow choice.

This sequence is an instructional model, not a universal law. Check its limitations against the contrasting case.

## Worked example

At a gathering someone says they started gardening. Ask 'What have you enjoyed growing?' rather than immediately switching to your own garden. After listening, share a brief related experience if appropriate.

### Contrasting case

Asking ten rapid personal questions without responding to answers can feel like interrogation.

## Guided practice

Turn 'Did you have a good weekend?' into an open prompt, then write a relevant follow-up. Model: 'What did you get up to?' followed by 'What made that walk enjoyable?' if they mention hiking.

Attempt the exercise before comparing to the model answer. Correct one specific gap.

## Independent transfer
