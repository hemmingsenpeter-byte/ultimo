# SOC-102 / L02 — Culture and context

## Orientation and retrieval

## Core teaching

Culture concerns shared and contested meanings, practices, expectations and institutions that people learn and negotiate in communities. Individuals participate in multiple contexts: families, workplaces, professions, languages, communities and online groups. No single national or demographic label tells you how an individual prefers to communicate. Social conventions also vary within communities, across generations and with the situation.

Context matters because the same observable action can serve different functions. Speaking directly might signal efficiency in one team and disregard for an agreed procedure in another. Silence may indicate listening, processing time, uncertainty, a role expectation or an ordinary preference. Neither direct nor indirect communication is intrinsically superior. The relevant question is how to communicate respectfully and accurately in the actual setting. UNESCO's intercultural dialogue framework treats engagement across differences as a way to improve understanding; it is not a catalogue of fixed cultural traits.

Cultural humility involves recognising limits in one's knowledge, checking relevant local practices and adapting with consent. It does not require abandoning safety, dignity, accessibility or legitimate obligations. A learner may need a translation, an accessible format or a clear description of a procedure. In Aotearoa New Zealand, appropriate tikanga and local guidance may be important in particular settings; consult the relevant hosts or invited expertise rather than claiming a universal protocol. Do not require people to represent an entire community or disclose their cultural identity.

## Framework

**Context–meaning–check–adapt**

Identify the actual setting, roles and purpose. Describe the observed practice without ranking it. Generate explanations grounded in the circumstances, including institutional rules or access needs. Ask a relevant host or participant about the procedure if appropriate. Adapt the communication while preserving safety and autonomy. Confirm whether the intended information was exchanged.

Text model: setting + roles + agreed purpose → possible convention → respectful verification. Identity is not a substitute for verification.

## Worked example

A fictional newcomer attends a committee meeting. Instead of interrupting to introduce a suggestion, participants wait for the chair to invite contributions. The newcomer initially interprets this as reluctance to speak. A plausible alternative is that the meeting uses an agreed turn-taking process. They can ask the organiser how contributions are normally submitted and whether written comments are welcome. If the procedure is accessible and legitimate, follow it. If it prevents a participant from raising an essential concern, identify an appropriate alternative channel. The goal is participation, not imitation of an assumed cultural personality.

### Contrasting case

Two people from the same community prefer different meeting styles. Their shared background does not resolve the difference. Ask each person what arrangement works, or agree a transparent procedure. Conversely, a practice that excludes a participant is not automatically acceptable because it is customary.

## Guided practice

**Compare fictional settings.** A: a formal meeting has an explicit speaking order. B: a social group changes topics quickly and informally. C: a participant requests written questions rather than spontaneous answers. Identify the contextual condition, a respectful adaptation and an inappropriate inference for each.

**Worked solution.** A: follow the agreed order or ask how to contribute; do not assume quiet participants lack ideas. B: use natural opportunities to join and permit others to finish; do not infer that rapid speakers are inherently more capable. C: offer a suitable written option where feasible; do not infer disinterest or diagnose the participant. If the arrangement conflicts with an actual duty, discuss an authorised alternative.

Create a short note for a newcomer explaining A without generalising about an entire community: state the meeting procedure, how to raise a concern and whom to ask for clarification.

Attempt before checking the worked solution. Identify a specific reasoning or communication correction and repeat using an equivalent fictional case. Assess observable task quality, not personality, popularity or another person's compliance.
