# SOC-102 / L03 — Group norms

**Draft — independent academic and qualified subject review pending.** Outcome: Apply group norms in the context of social awareness.

## Orientation and retrieval

Retrieve the context–meaning framework. A fictional team routinely replies to messages late, although its written agreement requires a timely response. Which of these describes behaviour and which describes expected conduct?

## Core teaching

A descriptive norm concerns what people commonly do or are perceived to do. An injunctive norm concerns what people approve, disapprove or expect. The distinction matters because actual practice and stated expectations can conflict. Cialdini and colleagues distinguish these concepts in their focus theory of normative conduct. Their research concerns how perceived norms can influence behaviour; it does not establish that a common behaviour is ethically justified.

Groups also have explicit rules, role responsibilities and informal conventions. Rules may be authorised and documented; informal norms may emerge from repeated interaction. A newcomer can observe a pattern without assuming it is obligatory. A group's stated agreement may not reflect the actual experience of every participant. The most visible or vocal members are not necessarily representative. An apparent norm can also arise from a small sample or from people feeling unable to disagree.

Social awareness includes recognising useful coordination practices, such as agreed turn-taking, while checking authority, safety and autonomy. Do not imitate discriminatory conduct, unsafe shortcuts or coercive rituals merely to fit in. A disagreement may be raised through an appropriate channel, or participation may be reconsidered when feasible. Some norms need to change rather than be learned. Assess the learner's ability to identify and evaluate norms, not willingness to conform or popularity.

## Framework

**Observe–distinguish–verify–decide**

Record a specific observed pattern. Separate descriptive practice, perceived approval, explicit rule and actual duty. Verify the rule with an authorised source if it matters. Check whether following the practice is safe, fair and compatible with rights and obligations. Choose to follow, clarify, propose a change or use appropriate support. Text example: most members do X; the group expects Y; the authorised requirement is Z. Those statements can differ.

## Worked example

A fictional volunteer group often starts meetings ten minutes late. Its published schedule says meetings begin at 6, and several members privately request punctuality because of transport arrangements. The descriptive pattern is lateness. The stated expectation is punctuality; approval of lateness cannot be inferred from the pattern. A newcomer can ask whether the start time has changed, and the organiser can clarify the actual arrangement. If the schedule is genuinely unrealistic, the group may agree a revision. The learner does not infer that everyone prefers lateness merely because it is common.

### Contrasting case

A workplace team routinely bypasses an authorised safety verification step. The common practice is not sufficient justification to follow it. The legitimate safety requirement remains relevant, and the learner should use appropriate reporting or supervisory procedures rather than treating conformity as social competence.

## Guided practice

**Classify the evidence.** In a fictional group, six of eight attendees bring notes; the chair explicitly requests preparation; a written rule requires protecting confidential information; one member shares private records with the group. Identify descriptive, injunctive and formal requirements, and a practice that should not be copied.

**Worked solution.** Bringing notes is a descriptive observation in that sample. The chair's request indicates an expectation, subject to actual role and access needs. Confidentiality is an explicit requirement. The single disclosure does not establish a legitimate norm and may breach the rule. Appropriate action depends on the actual procedure and authority. Avoid recording private details in the exercise.

Now imagine three of the eight members cannot use the usual note format. Propose a way to preserve preparation while allowing accessible alternatives. This tests adaptation of a legitimate goal rather than enforcing a superficial convention.

Attempt before checking the worked solution. Identify a specific reasoning or communication correction and repeat using an equivalent fictional case. Assess observable task quality, not personality, popularity or another person's compliance.

## Independent transfer

Analyse a fictional group's norms. Include two descriptive observations, one stated expectation, one authorised rule and one conflict between practice and duty. Propose a respectful clarification or improvement.
