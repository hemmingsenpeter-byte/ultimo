## Orientation and retrieval

Retrieve safe haven and secure base. Two fictional people have similar early circumstances but different current relationship patterns. Why is it inadequate to attribute later behaviour to one early event?

## Core teaching

Development concerns changes in capacities, relationships and environments over time. Attachment-related expectations may develop through repeated interactions, but caregiving occurs within broader systems: health, resources, cultural practices, housing, community support and child characteristics. Caregiver sensitivity generally involves noticing, interpreting and appropriately responding to signals. It does not mean perfect responsiveness, meeting every request immediately or requiring one exclusive caregiver.

A 2024 meta-analysis reported a pooled correlation of approximately 0.25 between caregiver sensitivity and attachment security across the included child-caregiver studies. This is evidence of an average positive association, not a deterministic rule or proof of causation. Correlation does not indicate that sensitivity accounts for all differences. Measurement and sample characteristics also matter. Do not translate a group association into a prediction of an individual child's future relationships.

Later experiences and current relationships may also contribute to patterns. A developmental explanation should consider timing, repeated observations, environmental resources and competing hypotheses rather than assuming an early experience fixes a person's future. Equally, a new relationship or communication technique cannot guarantee a specific psychological change. Multiple familiar caregivers and extended family or community relationships can be important. Where welfare or mental health concerns exist, seek qualified support rather than experimenting with caregiving or separation. No personal developmental history is needed for this lesson.

## Framework

**Developmental timeline–context–resources map**

Construct a fictional timeline showing early, later and current conditions. Mark observations separately from proposed mechanisms. Identify resources and constraints at each stage, then generate at least two explanations for the current behaviour. Identify one present support option without promising a particular developmental outcome. State what evidence would be needed to distinguish the hypotheses. Avoid drawing a single causal arrow from one childhood event to an adult behaviour.

## Worked example

A fictional adult finds it difficult to ask for help at a new workplace. Their given history includes reliable early family support and later criticism for asking questions. Current onboarding is unclear and no reliable contact is identified. Earlier learning and present organisational conditions are both plausible influences. A practical action is to clarify responsibilities and provide predictable, authorised ways to ask questions. This addresses a current barrier without determining an attachment orientation. If the person reports persistent distress or asks for clinical help, support access to qualified care instead of treating the timeline as an assessment.

### Contrasting case

Several familiar caregivers share responsibilities for a fictional child. The number of caregivers alone cannot establish insecurity. The quality, accessibility and continuity of relationships and the broader context matter.

## Guided practice

**Interpret a fictional timeline and a research statistic.** Early: stable support from familiar caregivers. Later: repeated school changes. Current: unfamiliar team and unclear expectations. Suggest two possible influences and a present support option. Explain whether a pooled correlation of 0.25 proves either explanation.

**Worked solution.** Earlier transitions might influence expectations about continuity; present role ambiguity might independently explain uncertainty. Neither is established. The group correlation concerns measured caregiver sensitivity and attachment security, not this worker or their current role. It does not validate a specific causal account. Clarifying the role and providing an available contact are feasible actions. More representative observations and appropriate voluntarily provided information would be needed for stronger inference.

A common mistake is to interpret r=0.25 as 25% of children being secure because of sensitivity. A correlation is not a percentage of individuals or a causal fraction. Explain the distinction in your own words.
