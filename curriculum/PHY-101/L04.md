# PHY-101 / L04 — Movement observation

## Core teaching

Movement observation begins with what can be seen: task, starting position, joint motion, timing and conditions. A description differs from a judgement about normality or injury. People vary in anatomy, experience and task strategy. A video cannot directly reveal tissue forces, pain causes or medical diagnoses. Use observations to ask useful questions and seek qualified guidance where needed; do not require painful or high-load demonstrations.

## Framework

Observe task → describe joint actions → identify context → state uncertainty → choose safe next step.

This sequence is an instructional model, not a universal law. Check its limitations against the contrasting case.

## Worked example

Two fictional learners stand from chairs using different trunk inclinations. Observation: one leans farther forward. Possible influences include chair height, body proportions or strategy; the video alone cannot identify a defect.

### Contrasting case

Labeling the more forward-leaning person injured from one clip overstates the evidence.

## Guided practice

Annotate a harmless everyday movement using a public instructional video or stick figures. Model: task, hip and knee actions, predominant planes, visible differences, uncertainty.

Attempt the exercise before comparing to the model answer. Correct one specific gap.
