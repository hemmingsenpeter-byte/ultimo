# PHY-101 / L02 — Joint anatomy

## Core teaching

Joints are connections between bones or other skeletal structures. Synovial joints have a cavity, articular cartilage and a capsule; supporting ligaments help constrain motion. Hinge-like joints such as the elbow primarily permit flexion and extension, while ball-and-socket joints such as the hip permit motion around several axes. Tendons transmit muscle force; ligaments generally connect bones. Joint shape, soft tissues and neuromuscular control influence available movement. Do not infer pathology from differences in range.

## Framework

Identify bones → joint type → permitted motion → supporting structures → task demand.

This sequence is an instructional model, not a universal law. Check its limitations against the contrasting case.

## Worked example

An elbow flexes to bring a cup toward the mouth; shoulder motion can also reposition the arm. The elbow hinge-like geometry and muscle action contribute to the task, while the shoulder has broader motion possibilities.

### Contrasting case

Calling all joints hinges ignores their different structures and degrees of freedom.

## Knowledge check

1. What do tendons transmit?
2. What is articular cartilage?
3. Why does a ball-and-socket allow more axes than a hinge?

<details><summary>Answers (open after attempting)</summary>

1. Muscle force to attached structures.
2. Tissue covering joint surfaces in synovial joints.
3. Its geometry permits broader rotational motion.
