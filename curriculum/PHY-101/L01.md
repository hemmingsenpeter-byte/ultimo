## Core teaching

Anatomical position provides a shared reference for describing movement. The sagittal plane divides left and right, frontal divides anterior and posterior, and transverse divides superior and inferior portions. Flexion and extension generally occur in sagittal motion, abduction and adduction often in frontal motion, and axial rotation often in transverse motion. Actual tasks can involve multiple planes; a camera angle is not a movement plane. Use joint-specific description rather than assuming every body moves identically.

## Framework

Identify body reference → joint → motion → predominant plane → note secondary motion.

This sequence is an instructional model, not a universal law. Check its limitations against the contrasting case.

## Worked example

A sit-to-stand involves hip and knee extension predominantly in the sagittal plane. Raising an arm sideways involves shoulder abduction, typically frontal. Turning the trunk involves rotation around a vertical axis, predominantly transverse.

### Contrasting case

A diagonal reach combines movements across planes; assigning it to exactly one plane may oversimplify.
