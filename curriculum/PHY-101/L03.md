# PHY-101 / L03 — Muscle leverage

**Draft — independent review pending.** Outcome: Apply muscle leverage in the context of anatomy and biomechanics.

## Orientation and retrieval

Before reading, attempt the first knowledge check without notes. Recall Joint anatomy and connect it to this topic.

## Core teaching

Muscles exert forces that may produce rotation about joints. Torque equals force times perpendicular moment arm in the simple static model. A longer external moment arm increases external torque for the same force. Muscles may act as agonists, antagonists or stabilisers depending on the task. Actual joint loads include muscle forces, acceleration and multiple segments, so a simple lever calculation is not a clinical assessment or personalised training prescription.

## Framework

Mark pivot → force direction → perpendicular distance → calculate torque → note model assumptions.

This sequence is an instructional model, not a universal law. Check its limitations against the contrasting case.

## Worked example

A hypothetical 20 N downward force acts 0.2 m horizontally from an elbow pivot: external torque is 4 N·m. At 0.4 m the torque is 8 N·m. The force is unchanged, but the moment arm doubled.

### Contrasting case

Using full limb length when the force is parallel to the limb gives the wrong perpendicular moment arm.

## Guided practice

Calculate external torque for 15 N at 0.1 and 0.3 m. Model: 1.5 and 4.5 N·m; the second is three times the first. Use a diagram, not a loaded exercise.

Attempt the exercise before comparing to the model answer. Correct one specific gap.

## Independent transfer

Draw a different lever example and identify why a simplified external torque does not reveal exact joint tissue forces.

## Knowledge check

1. What is a moment arm?
2. If force stays constant and moment arm doubles?
3. Can external torque alone diagnose injury?

<details><summary>Answers (open after attempting)</summary>

1. Perpendicular distance from pivot to force line.
2. Torque doubles in the simple model.
3. No.
