# INT-106 / L01 — Stocks and flows

**Draft — independent review pending.** Outcome: Explain stocks and flows in the context of systems thinking.

## Orientation and retrieval

Imagine a support queue containing 30 open jobs. Twelve arrive and eight close during a day. What changes, and what is measured in jobs per day?

## Core teaching

A stock is an accumulated quantity measured at a moment, such as water in a reservoir, outstanding jobs or inventory. Flows change the stock over time and have units of quantity per time. A discrete accounting model states next stock = current stock + inflow − outflow, where flows are totals for the interval. If they are rates, multiply by the interval duration. Stocks can increase even while outflows increase if inflows grow faster. A stable stock can hide substantial turnover when both flows are equal. The system boundary determines whether a transfer is an inflow, outflow or internal movement. Specify whether items can be duplicated, lost or transformed; simple conservation applies only to the defined quantity and accounting boundary.

## Framework

Draw a box for the stock and arrows for flows entering and leaving. Label units, time step and initial condition. Write the balance equation, then test the zero-flow and equal-flow cases. Compare implied stock with observed values before adding complexity.

## Worked example

An order queue starts with 30 outstanding jobs, receives 12, and closes 8. End stock is 34. Next day arrivals fall to 10 and closures rise to 9, so it grows to 35. Although both operational changes are favourable, arrivals still exceed completions. To reduce backlog, the net flow must be negative for a period.

### Contrasting case

Daily enquiries are a flow, not the number of customers currently served. A report that subtracts a daily rate directly from a monthly total without harmonising time intervals is dimensionally inconsistent.

## Guided practice

Start a fictional stock of 60 library holds. Week one adds 25 and resolves 35; week two adds 40 and resolves 30. Model: stocks 50 then 60. Explain why the final stock hides the week's activity. Draw the three stock observations and label weekly flow totals.

Attempt before reading the model; compare your reasoning and correct a specific gap. Record assumptions and ask what would change your conclusion.

## Independent transfer

Map a second stock such as available equipment or unresolved forms. Specify boundary, units, initial condition and two time-step balances. Identify a missing flow that could break the equation.
