# INT-106 / L02 — Feedback loops

**Draft — independent review pending.** Outcome: Compare and distinguish feedback loops in the context of systems thinking.

## Orientation and retrieval

Recall the stock equation. Now consider how the size of a queue may itself change the rate at which jobs are processed.

## Core teaching

A feedback loop exists when an effect returns through a chain of influences to alter its original cause. Reinforcing loops amplify a change; balancing loops tend to oppose a change relative to a target or constraint. These labels concern causal structure rather than moral desirability. Reinforcing feedback can amplify adoption or deterioration. Balancing feedback can stabilise a desired process or preserve an undesirable status quo. Mark each link positive if increasing its cause tends to increase its effect, holding other relevant factors fixed, and negative if it tends to decrease it. A loop with an odd number of negative links is balancing; an even number is reinforcing in this simplified signed representation. A real system can contain many loops whose dominance changes over time.

## Framework

Use a signed causal loop map. Select variables that can increase or decrease; draw arrows with signs; close the path; identify the implied loop polarity; then check the story against a hypothetical increase in the starting variable. A causal diagram is a hypothesis, not proof of the arrows.

## Worked example

A fictional training programme improves operator skill, which reduces rework, which frees time for further training: the two negative links create a reinforcing loop. Separately, increased queue size triggers more assigned staff, which increases completion rate and reduces queue size: a balancing loop. The first may weaken if staff run out of training capacity; the second may be constrained by budgets.

### Contrasting case

A sequence from advertising to enquiries to sales is not a loop unless sales affect something that feeds back into advertising or enquiries. A diagram of correlations is not automatically a causal model.

## Guided practice

Map increasing late jobs → increased overtime → increased fatigue → more errors → increased late jobs. All arrows are positive in the stated fictional mechanism: reinforcing. Add a balancing path: more late jobs → extra capacity → fewer late jobs. Identify the evidence needed to verify each arrow and potential delay.

Attempt before reading the model; compare your reasoning and correct a specific gap. Record assumptions and ask what would change your conclusion.

## Independent transfer

Build one reinforcing and one balancing loop around the same stock from L01. Explain how a resource limit could alter which loop dominates.
