# INT-101 / L01 — Learning versus familiarity

**Draft — independent review pending.** Outcome: Explain learning versus familiarity in the context of learning how to learn.

## Orientation and retrieval

Before reading, attempt the first knowledge check without notes. Write a baseline explanation and mark uncertainties.

## Core teaching

Recognition is the feeling that material is familiar when it is in front of you. Recall is producing it without the material, while understanding involves explaining relationships and applying them in a new situation. These are distinct tests. Familiarity can increase during rereading without demonstrating durable learning. Retrieval practice has strong research support for delayed retention, but a correct answer can still be memorised without understanding. Use a novel problem as a second test. Separate immediate performance from learning that survives a delay.

## Framework

Read → close → reconstruct → check → apply. Write the original idea without notes, compare to the source, correct errors, then use it in a new case.

This sequence is an instructional model, not a universal law. Check its limitations against the contrasting case.

## Worked example

A learner reads about opportunity cost and recognises the definition. Asked to choose between a free evening of study and paid work, they identify the best forgone alternative rather than listing every possible alternative. That new application is stronger evidence than recognition.

### Contrasting case

Repeating a definition while the book is open may reflect familiarity; it is not proof of recall.

## Guided practice

Read a short paragraph on a new concept, close it and explain it; then invent an example. Compare with the source and correct one omission. A successful response includes the main idea, supporting relation and a genuinely new example.

Attempt the exercise before comparing to the model answer. Correct one specific gap.

## Independent transfer

Select an unfamiliar concept from a different field and test it again tomorrow without notes; record both recall and application.

## Knowledge check

1. What distinguishes recall from recognition?
2. Why delay a test?
3. What does a novel problem test?

<details><summary>Answers (open after attempting)</summary>

1. Recall reconstructs without seeing the answer; recognition identifies a presented answer.
2. Immediate fluency can exaggerate durable retention.
3. Whether the idea can be applied beyond the original example.

</details>
