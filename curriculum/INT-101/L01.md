# Learning versus familiarity

**Objective:** Distinguish recognition from recall and application.

Read a short text on an unfamiliar topic for eight minutes. Close it and explain the central idea and two supporting details in your own words. Reopen it and mark omissions. Familiarity is recognition; recall requires reconstructing knowledge without the source. Understanding also requires explaining and using it in a new case. Immediate fluency can mislead: use a delayed test before concluding that you have learned something.

Example: recognising a definition of opportunity cost does not prove that you can apply it to choosing between two job offers. Write a new scenario and solve it without notes.

**Knowledge check:** You reread a chapter and it seems easy. What evidence would better establish retention? Explain why a delayed closed-book test is preferable.

**Practice:** Choose a real skill you want to acquire; write a baseline explanation and one measurable target. Keep your work privately.
