## Core teaching

A learning system converts a vague ambition into a testable goal, a baseline and manageable practice. It should specify what evidence would count as improvement before study begins. Use a small number of questions, spaced attempts, corrective feedback and a novel application. Keep an error log to distinguish misunderstanding from forgetting. Rest and workload are constraints, not moral tests. The seven-day cycle is an Ultimo design exercise, not a validated certification of mastery.

## Framework

Goal → baseline → practice → feedback → delayed test → transfer → revise. Review the weakest element before adding more content.

This sequence is an instructional model, not a universal law. Check its limitations against the contrasting case.

## Worked example

Goal: compare simple and compound interest. Baseline: confuse the formulas. Sessions: explain both, calculate an example, retrieve the distinction later, then solve a new example. The learner notices that they can calculate but cannot explain why the curves diverge and revises the plan.

### Contrasting case

A schedule that logs two hours of reading but contains no test or application cannot establish improvement.

## Guided practice

Choose one useful concept; set a baseline, five questions, three sessions and a delayed task. Model: a measurable goal names a behaviour such as explaining a concept and solving two unfamiliar problems.

Attempt the exercise before comparing to the model answer. Correct one specific gap.

## Independent transfer

Complete the seven-day capstone and repeat it on another topic before claiming independent competence.
