# INT-101 / L02 — Retrieval and spaced practice

## Orientation and retrieval

Before reading, attempt the first knowledge check without notes. Recall Learning versus familiarity and connect it to this topic.

## Core teaching

Retrieval strengthens access to studied material by requiring an attempt to remember, whereas spacing distributes attempts over time. In research on learning techniques, practice testing and distributed practice show broad utility. Their effect depends on content, timing, prior knowledge and the test. Use corrective feedback so mistakes do not remain unchallenged. A schedule of day 1, 3, 7 and 30 is a starting plan, not an empirically universal optimum. A card asking why or how can test understanding better than a card containing only a definition.

## Framework

Question → attempt without notes → check → correct → reschedule. Increase interval when retrieval is reliable, shorten it after errors.

This sequence is an instructional model, not a universal law. Check its limitations against the contrasting case.

## Worked example

Maya studies five terms on Monday and retrieves them Tuesday. She misses two, corrects them and repeats Thursday. On Sunday she explains the terms in a new case. The difficult items receive more attention.

### Contrasting case

Reading the same five terms four times in one sitting can feel easy but does not provide four spaced retrieval attempts.

## Guided practice

Write five questions about a paragraph. Attempt answers, mark correct/partial/incorrect, correct mistakes and schedule next attempts. Model: a missed cause-effect question becomes a next-day review question rather than a highlighted sentence.

Attempt the exercise before comparing to the model answer. Correct one specific gap.
