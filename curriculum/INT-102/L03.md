# INT-102 / L03 — Induction and fallacies

**Draft — independent review pending.** Outcome: Apply induction and fallacies in the context of logic and argument.

## Orientation and retrieval

Recall the countermodel test. Now imagine inspecting five deliveries that all arrived on time. How confident should you be about the next hundred?

## Core teaching

Inductive reasoning moves beyond what the evidence guarantees. A sample, pattern or analogy supports a broader claim to a greater or lesser degree. Strength depends on how observations were selected, their relevance, variation and the presence of rival explanations. Five observations from one convenient site may tell us less about a network than a representative sample across sites. Induction is indispensable to everyday learning, but conclusions must remain revisable. A hasty generalisation extrapolates beyond the support available. A false dilemma presents options as exhaustive when others exist. An ad hominem substitutes an irrelevant attack on the person for an evaluation of the argument; relevant expertise or conflicts of interest can still matter to testimony. Fallacy names are diagnostic shorthand, not a substitute for demonstrating the defective inference. A flawed argument does not prove its conclusion false.

## Framework

Use SCOPE: state the generalisation; check selection of observations; observe variation and base rates; propose alternative explanations; express a calibrated conclusion. Write a stronger and weaker formulation, and specify what additional information would justify the stronger one. This original checklist is a practical aid, not a statistical significance test.

## Worked example

Four of five customers interviewed after a product demonstration say they would buy. A founder claims 80% of all potential customers will purchase. The four responses describe this small group, but selection after a demonstration, courtesy bias and the difference between intention and payment weaken the extrapolation. A defensible conclusion is that the concept deserves a broader test using actual purchase behaviour.

### Contrasting case

A well-designed survey can still yield uncertain estimates, but a probability sample with transparent non-response and measurement limitations provides better grounds for population inference than five hand-picked enthusiasts.

## Guided practice

Analyse: ‘Two colleagues disliked the new roster, so everyone hates it; either cancel it or accept resignations.’ Model: the first step is a hasty generalisation, the second a false dilemma. Interview across shifts, examine alternatives such as a trial or shift swaps, and distinguish dissatisfaction from intention to resign. Write a revised recommendation with appropriate uncertainty.

Attempt the task before reading the model; compare your reasoning, correct one concrete gap and retry.

## Independent transfer

Find a fictional headline making a broad prediction. List the population, observed sample, missing data and one plausible rival explanation; rewrite the conclusion as an appropriately limited claim.
