# INT-102 / L04 — Steelmanning

**Draft — independent review pending.** Outcome: Evaluate and justify decisions about steelmanning in the context of logic and argument.

## Orientation and retrieval

Write a position you disagree with about a fictional community policy. Before criticising it, ask whether someone who holds it would recognise your description.

## Core teaching

Steelmanning is a practical extension of charitable interpretation: reconstruct a position in its strongest reasonable form before assessing it. The aim is to test the real disagreement, not to manufacture an impressive opponent. Keep the speaker’s actual position distinct from improvements you supply yourself. A fair reconstruction preserves their conclusion, central reasons, constraints and relevant qualifications. A straw man weakens or distorts a position to make it easier to attack. Charity does not require accepting false premises, tolerating abuse, giving equal weight to unequal evidence, or supplying a completely different argument. Once you have clarified the competing cases, locate the crux: a factual belief, value judgement or assumption that materially changes the decision. People can share facts and disagree about priorities; in that case a new statistic may not resolve the conflict.

## Framework

Use the two-sided map: position A and its strongest stated reasons; position B and its strongest stated reasons; shared ground; decisive disagreement; evidence or trade-off that could change each side. Mark enhancements as proposed rather than attributed. Finish with your provisional judgement and one condition under which you would revise it.

## Worked example

A library proposes closing an hour later. Supporters cite access for shift workers and students; opponents cite staffing cost and existing budget pressure. The strongest opposition is not hostility to readers but concern about displacing other useful services. The crux is whether the extra hour attracts enough additional use to justify the opportunity cost. A limited trial with transparent measures could inform both sides, provided staffing arrangements are acceptable.

### Contrasting case

Calling the opponents ‘people who hate education’ misrepresents their argument. Conversely, inventing a sophisticated legal objection they never made and presenting it as their view is not faithful steelmanning.

## Guided practice

Map both sides of a fictional proposal to replace printed notices with email. Model: email reduces printing and makes updates rapid; print reaches people without reliable digital access. Shared aim: timely communication. Crux: whether a hybrid system can serve both groups at acceptable cost. Propose a test and state what result would alter your recommendation.

Attempt the task before reading the model; compare your reasoning, correct one concrete gap and retry.

## Independent transfer

Prepare a two-sided argument map for a new decision about a shared facility. Include the actual claim, charitable reformulation, unresolved premise, strongest objection to your own position and revised judgement.

## Knowledge check

1. What distinguishes steelmanning from a straw man?
2. Must you agree with a charitably reconstructed argument?
3. What is a crux?

<details><summary>Answers (open after attempting)</summary>

1. Steelmanning reconstructs the strongest reasonable version; a straw man distorts or weakens the position. Check fidelity to the original claim.
2. No; fair interpretation precedes evaluation, not endorsement. Explain the strongest remaining objection.
3. A disagreement or assumption whose resolution could materially change a judgement. Identify a specific fact or trade-off.
