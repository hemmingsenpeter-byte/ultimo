# EMO-108 / L04 — Compassionate limits

**Draft — independent academic and qualified subject review pending.** Outcome: Evaluate and justify decisions about compassionate limits in the context of empathy.

## Orientation and retrieval

## Core teaching

Compassion here refers to concern for another person's welfare and willingness to respond appropriately. It does not require absorbing another person's distress, becoming their sole support provider or accepting every request. Limits can make assistance realistic, reliable and suited to competence. Ordinary care may include listening, practical assistance or helping locate other resources. Professional roles may carry additional duties, so identify actual responsibilities rather than applying a generic peer script.

A feasible offer identifies what is available, when, and what exceeds the helper's role. Ask whether it is wanted. A refusal can recognise a legitimate need while communicating real capacity limits. Do not promise unlimited availability, clinical care, confidentiality beyond the role or guaranteed outcomes. Multiple support routes can be useful when a concern exceeds ordinary peer assistance. Helpers can also seek their own appropriate support while minimising unnecessary disclosure of another person's information.

Safety changes the response. Immediate danger requires emergency assistance, while persistent or serious impairment may warrant qualified professional care. Empathy does not require staying in a threatening interaction, managing another person's emotions or exposing oneself to harm. A person experiencing coercion is not responsible for preventing harm through perfect communication. The goal is appropriate, consent-based support that preserves actual duties, autonomy and competence. This lesson is not an assessment of personal compassion, caregiving fitness or mental health.

## Framework

**Need–capacity–offer–route–review**

Identify the need and urgency. Check actual role, duties, available time and competence. Offer specific voluntary help or communicate a limit. Identify alternatives or qualified support when the need exceeds capacity. Agree follow-up only when wanted and feasible. Immediate danger requires emergency assistance.

Text model: need + urgency + capacity → proportionate offer or referral. Do not make the helper the sole source of assistance.

## Worked example

A fictional friend asks for nightly conversations after a difficult period. Their peer has early work duties and cannot reliably promise that availability. A bounded response could be: 'I can talk for twenty minutes this evening. I can't commit to every night, but if you'd like, I can help find another support option.' This recognises the request without making a promise that is unlikely to be kept. If the friend reports persistent functional difficulty, qualified care may be appropriate. Immediate danger would require emergency assistance. The peer can continue ordinary contact if wanted and safe; the limit does not establish indifference.

### Contrasting case

A helper insists they must solve someone else's clinical difficulties alone to demonstrate compassion. This exceeds their competence and may create unreliable support. Connecting the person to suitable qualified help can be more appropriate than taking over.

## Guided practice

**Compare three fictional requests.** A: help rescheduling an appointment. B: a request for ongoing clinical counselling from a peer. C: immediate danger. Identify a compassionate response, role boundary and alternative route.

**Worked solution.** A: offer feasible practical assistance with consent, without assuming control of the appointment. B: acknowledge the need, explain the peer's role and offer help locating an appropriate qualified provider. C: contact emergency services promptly rather than relying on ordinary conversation. In each case, protect privacy and avoid promises beyond capacity.

Now revise A when the helper has no available time. A truthful refusal plus an alternative contact, if known and wanted, can still be appropriate. Evaluate the response by feasibility, reliability and respect for choice, not the amount of self-sacrifice. If an actual role obligation exists, use the authorised handover or escalation process.

Attempt before checking the worked solution. Correct one specific reasoning or communication gap and repeat with a novel fictional case. Assess task quality, not personal emotional experiences.
