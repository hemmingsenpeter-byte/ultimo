# INT-104 / L02 — Controls and confounding

**Draft — independent review pending.** Outcome: Compare and distinguish controls and confounding in the context of scientific reasoning.

## Orientation and retrieval

Recall your hypothesis and outcome measure. If an improved process is introduced only to the most experienced employees, what besides the process could explain their performance?

## Core teaching

A comparison estimates what would have happened under an alternative condition. A control group or baseline provides a reference against which an intervention is evaluated. A confounder is a factor associated with the exposure or treatment and affecting the outcome in a way that can distort the estimated relationship. In an observational comparison, more experienced staff may preferentially use a new tool and also work faster regardless of the tool. Random assignment can reduce systematic allocation differences in expectation, although small randomised groups can still be imbalanced. Random assignment is not the same as random sampling: one concerns treatment allocation, the other selection from a population. Standardising tasks, measurement and instructions reduces unwanted variation; masking outcome evaluators where feasible can reduce differential measurement. No single control solves all design problems.

## Framework

Build a comparison matrix: treatment and reference; same outcome measurement; factors to hold fixed; factors to randomise or balance; possible missing data; limits on generalisation. Draw a confounding fork in words: experience → choice of tool; experience → performance. The association between tool and performance may reflect experience.

## Worked example

A team compares two file-search systems. Experienced employees use the new system and junior staff the old. The new system appears quicker, but experience is a plausible confounder. A safer design using fictional files randomly assigns comparable tasks and alternates the order across participants, measures the same accuracy and time, and checks for learning carryover. If carryover is substantial, separate randomly assigned groups may be preferable.

### Contrasting case

Holding the file set constant while giving one group extra coaching controls document difficulty but leaves unequal instruction. A single controlled variable does not guarantee a fair comparison.
