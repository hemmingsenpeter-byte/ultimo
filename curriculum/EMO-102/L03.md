## Orientation and retrieval

## Core teaching

Psychological measurement attempts to represent a construct, such as a trait, using observations or responses. The construct is not directly observed in the same way as the length of a table. An instrument operationalises it through items, scoring procedures and an interpretation. Reliability concerns consistency under relevant conditions; examples include consistency across related items, repeated administrations or raters. Validity concerns whether evidence supports the interpretation and use of a score for a specified purpose. A measure can be consistent while measuring a different construct, or unsuitable for a decision even if useful for research.

Self-report is one measurement approach. Responses can be affected by wording, interpretation, reference group, context, response styles, incentives or accessibility. Other-report measures introduce the observer's perspective and opportunities to observe behaviour. No instrument removes all uncertainty. Norms describe scores in a specified reference group; percentiles are relative ranks, not percentages of a personality possessed. A score of 70th percentile would mean a position relative to the norm distribution, not 70% extraverted or a 70% probability of success. Changes in population, language and use may require new evidence.

Responsible interpretation asks what claim is supported, in which population, under what conditions and with what error or limitations. The International Test Commission emphasises reliability and validity evidence for intended use. Evidence for an inventory used in research does not automatically establish suitability for recruitment, clinical diagnosis or relationship matching. An entertaining quiz can be a conversation prompt while lacking evidence for consequential assessment. Do not administer or score a real psychological test in this course; critique fictional claims and published descriptions rather than evaluating learners.

## Framework

**Instrument claim audit**

Record the claimed construct, intended use, target population, item and scoring approach, reliability evidence, validity evidence, norms and limitations. Separate evidence provided from evidence missing. Ask what alternative construct the items might capture and whether the decision has meaningful consequences. Conclude with a narrow justified use, a conditional use needing evidence, or rejection of the proposed use.

Text model: construct → operationalisation → score → supported interpretation → proposed use. Every arrow needs evidence.

## Worked example

A fictional quiz asks six questions about preference for busy social events, gives a 'natural leader' label and advertises high repeatability. Even if participants answer consistently, the items may primarily reflect sociability rather than leadership skill. The label requires evidence that the measured construct predicts relevant leadership outcomes in the intended population. That evidence is absent. A defensible use might be an informal discussion of preferences, explicitly not an assessment. A hiring panel would need properly validated, relevant and fair procedures and qualified review, not a quiz label. Consistency alone cannot bridge the gap between observed preferences and the advertised claim.

### Contrasting case

A published research inventory with evidence for a specific trait interpretation is not therefore invalid simply because it uses self-report. Its usefulness depends on the evidence, purpose and population. The correct response is a proportionate audit, not rejection of all questionnaires.

## Guided practice

**Critique a fictional instrument.** An app claims that a ten-item score measures 'relationship maturity'. It reports that 80 of 100 users received the same category one week later, but provides no item-development evidence, reference population or relationship outcome study. Identify what the result does and does not establish.

**Worked solution.** The 80% category agreement is a descriptive consistency result for that sample; it does not establish item quality, accuracy, stability under other conditions or validity for relationship decisions. The category thresholds and missing cases matter. Ask for evidence defining the construct, showing score interpretation and supporting the intended use. Do not infer clinical maturity or partner compatibility. The correct verdict is insufficient evidence for consequential use.

Attempt before checking the model. Identify one specific correction and apply it to an equivalent fictional case. Assess reasoning, evidence, safe boundaries and task quality rather than personal characteristics or emotional states.
