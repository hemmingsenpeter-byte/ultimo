# SOC-108 / L02 — Low-pressure requests

**Draft — independent academic and subject review pending.** Outcome: Compare and distinguish low-pressure requests in the context of networking.

## Orientation and retrieval

Retrieve EMO-106 on assertive requests and SOC-102 on clarifying questions. A fictional professional wants advice from a former colleague. What distinguishes a clear low-pressure request from an open-ended demand on their time?

## Core teaching

A low-pressure request states a legitimate purpose, a specific action and a feasible way to accept, decline or suggest an alternative. It reduces ambiguity without using guilt, inflated urgency, status or false familiarity. In networking, a useful request might ask for a short discussion about a public topic, a reference to an available resource or feedback on one defined question. The recipient should be able to understand the request without first committing to an unspecified obligation.

Time and information have costs. A request for a short conversation is different from asking someone to prepare a detailed report, share confidential information or make introductions. Explain the relevant context and avoid disguising a sales pitch or job request as purely social conversation. If the purpose is commercial, say so honestly. A recipient's role may limit what they can provide, and a previously friendly interaction does not establish permission for repeated outreach.

Low pressure does not mean apologising excessively or hiding the purpose. A concise direct message can be considerate. Offer a suitable written alternative when a meeting is unnecessary. Respect a refusal or lack of response; a follow-up may be appropriate in some contexts, but should not become repeated pursuit. Do not use automation to send unwanted bulk messages or obtain private contact details. Applicable communication, privacy and organisational rules must be respected. The assessed skill is clarity, proportionality and autonomy, not response rates.

## Framework

**Context–purpose–specific ask–choice**

State how the contact is relevant without exaggerating the relationship. Explain the actual purpose. Request one bounded action and specify a realistic time or format. Make refusal easy, avoid unnecessary information and identify any applicable role constraints. Stop if declined; consider a single proportionate follow-up only where appropriate.

Example structure: 'I'm exploring X. Your public work on Y is relevant. Would you be open to a brief discussion about Z? A written reference would also be helpful if easier.'

## Worked example

A fictional learner is exploring how community organisations manage accessible venues. They write to a former colleague: 'I'm learning how organisations assess venue access. I remember you worked on event operations. Would you be open to a 15-minute conversation about the general process sometime next week? A public resource recommendation would also be helpful if easier. No problem if you're unavailable.' The request is bounded and transparent. It does not ask for internal documents, confidential cases or a referral. If the recipient declines, the learner can use public guidance or another appropriate route without arguing.

### Contrasting case

A fictional message says, 'If you really care about helping new professionals, you will introduce me to your director today.' This uses guilt, imposes an unsupported deadline and requests access to a third person without their consent. A smaller transparent request or a public route is more appropriate.

## Guided practice

**Rewrite three fictional requests.** A: 'Can I pick your brain sometime?' B: 'Send me your company's private customer list; it'll help my research.' C: 'I need you to get me an interview urgently.' Identify the purpose, burden, authority and response choice.

**Worked solution.** A: specify the relevant topic and a bounded discussion, with a written alternative. B: do not request private customer information; ask whether public sector resources are available. C: if appropriate, ask about the published recruitment process or whether the recipient would be comfortable sharing general advice; do not demand influence. Do not conceal a genuine job-search goal.

Compare a 15-minute discussion with a request for several hours of unpaid analysis. The latter has a larger burden and may require a formal arrangement. A precise ask is not automatically reasonable merely because it is politely worded.

Attempt before checking the model. Identify a specific correction and repeat with an equivalent fictional case. Explain why the action fits the actual context and what would change the decision.
