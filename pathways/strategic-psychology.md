# Ultimo elective — Strategic psychology, power and human behaviour

**Status:** proposed curriculum blueprint; not authored lessons, validated psychological training or an accredited qualification.

**Scope:** eight specialist modules, 32 lesson briefs, eight assessment artefacts and one integrative capstone. Indicative 52 hours (eight modules of six hours plus four hours for the capstone), excluding extended reading. Self-paced. Uses existing faculties rather than a seventeenth faculty. No changes to canonical course IDs, core graduation requirements or existing rubrics.

## Purpose and entry

Develop strategic literacy: understand how influence, status, incentives, informal power and coercion shape human choices; recognise manipulative behaviour; make effective decisions and negotiate under uncertainty; exercise power with accountability. The course takes uncomfortable mechanisms seriously without endorsing deception, intimidation, exploitation of vulnerability, or bypassing another person's consent. Study harmful mechanisms through analysis and protective responses, not live experiments on unsuspecting people.

Recommended preparation: [INT-104 scientific reasoning](../curriculum/INT-104/syllabus.md), [INT-105 cognitive biases](../curriculum/INT-105/syllabus.md), [EMO-102 personality](../curriculum/EMO-102/syllabus.md), [EMO-106 boundaries](../curriculum/EMO-106/syllabus.md), [COM-103 listening and questioning](../curriculum/COM-103/syllabus.md), and [PHI-105 ethics](../curriculum/PHI-105/syllabus.md). Complete these or equivalent learning first; canonical prerequisites remain in the linked syllabuses. The SP labels below are elective-local identifiers, not registered course or competency IDs.

## SP-01 — Behavioural observation and profiling literacy

**Canonical anchors:** [SOC-102 social awareness](../curriculum/SOC-102/syllabus.md), EMO-102, COM-103, INT-104. **Reading:** Hughes, *Six-Minute X-Ray*; Greene, *The Laws of Human Nature*; Bond and DePaulo (2006).

| Lesson | Outcome | Guided practice |
|---|---|---|
| 01 — Observation versus interpretation | Separate visible behaviour, context and inference | Annotate a fictional meeting transcript in three columns; mark unsupported conclusions |
| 02 — Nonverbal behaviour and uncertainty | Explain why one gesture cannot establish a mental state or lie | Compare two plausible accounts of a pause; identify what additional evidence would distinguish them |
| 03 — Motivation and personality | Distinguish a stated preference from a speculative personality judgement | Classify statements by evidential strength and propose neutral clarifying questions |
| 04 — Calibration and verification | Update a hypothesis when contradictory information arrives | Revisit a fictional case after receiving new facts; document confidence and revision |

**Assessment:** observation-and-inference portfolio. Criteria: distinguish facts from hypotheses; identify alternatives; seek disconfirming evidence; respect privacy and avoid diagnosis. Contrast observation of hesitation with mistaken certainty that a person is deceptive.

## SP-02 — Human motives, interpersonal games and self-deception

**Canonical anchors:** EMO-102, INT-105, [EMO-108 empathy](../curriculum/EMO-108/syllabus.md). **Reading:** Greene, *The Laws of Human Nature*; Berne, *Games People Play*; Simon, *In Sheep's Clothing*.

| Lesson | Outcome | Guided practice |
|---|---|---|
| 01 — Needs, incentives and stated intentions | Compare distinct explanations for a decision without assuming hidden intent | Model competing explanations of a fictional colleague's behaviour |
| 02 — Bias and motivated reasoning | Identify confirmation bias and self-serving interpretation | Rewrite an accusation as a testable claim and name contrary evidence |
| 03 — Interpersonal games | Analyse recurring conflict as an interaction pattern rather than a diagnosis | Diagram a fictional argument, identifying claims, responses and alternative explanations |
| 04 — Accountability and boundaries | Distinguish a disagreement from repeated boundary violations | Choose proportionate responses to three contrasting cases |

**Assessment:** interaction analysis with alternative hypotheses and an assertive, non-retaliatory response plan. Criteria: grounded observations; accurate use of concepts; plausible alternatives; respect for boundaries. Berne and Greene are interpretive frameworks, not diagnostic instruments.

## SP-03 — Persuasion and decision architecture

**Canonical anchors:** INT-105, [COM-105 rhetoric](../curriculum/COM-105/syllabus.md), [BUS-105 marketing](../curriculum/BUS-105/syllabus.md). **Reading:** Cialdini, *Influence*, *Pre-Suasion*; Rushkoff, *Coercion*.

| Lesson | Outcome | Guided practice |
|---|---|---|
| 01 — Reciprocity, authority and social proof | Identify influence mechanisms and their limitations | Audit fictional marketing statements for supporting evidence |
| 02 — Attention and framing | Distinguish truthful framing from material omission | Compare two descriptions of the same product; recover missing material information |
| 03 — Scarcity and commitment | Recognise how urgency and prior commitments can affect choices | Compare a genuine inventory limit with an unsupported deadline; specify verification |
| 04 — Persuasive argument and informed choice | Present a defensible proposal with clear trade-offs and freedom to decline | Draft an evidence-based recommendation, then invite counterarguments |

**Assessment:** persuasion audit and revised proposal. Criteria: correct mechanism identification; truthful substantiation; fair alternatives and risks; voluntary decision-making. Do not measure success by obtaining compliance.

## SP-04 — Dark psychology and coercion literacy

**Canonical anchors:** EMO-106, [COM-107 difficult conversations](../curriculum/COM-107/syllabus.md), PHI-105. **Reading:** Simon, *In Sheep's Clothing*; Konnikova, *The Confidence Game*; Rushkoff, *Coercion*; Lifton, *Thought Reform and the Psychology of Totalism*; Hughes, *The Ellipsis Manual* (critical reading).

| Lesson | Outcome | Guided practice |
|---|---|---|
| 01 — Manipulation, persuasion and coercion | Distinguish informed influence, deceptive pressure and threats using consent and autonomy | Classify fictional exchanges and justify boundaries |
| 02 — Deception and trust exploitation | Identify inconsistencies and independent verification options in a suspicious offer | Audit a fictional unsolicited investment pitch without engaging its sender |
| 03 — Coercive control and psychological pressure | Recognise patterns of isolation, intimidation and constrained choice; avoid diagnosis from a single incident | Analyse a fictional case for safety concerns and appropriate support routes |
| 04 — Counter-manipulation and exit | Choose proportionate defensive responses, including delaying decisions and seeking independent advice | Practise declining a high-pressure request and documenting an incident in a simulation |

**Assessment:** protective decision plan. Criteria: recognise material risk without overclaiming intent; verify independently; maintain agency; select appropriate support or escalation. No exercises that induce distress, threaten others or simulate abuse without informed participation. Where danger exists, prioritise safety and qualified support rather than confrontation.

## SP-05 — Organisational power and political intelligence

**Canonical anchors:** [LEA-105 organisational psychology](../curriculum/LEA-105/syllabus.md), [LEA-106 power and institutions](../curriculum/LEA-106/syllabus.md), [SOC-108 networking](../curriculum/SOC-108/syllabus.md). **Reading:** Pfeffer, *Power* and *7 Rules of Power*; Fairholm, *Organizational Power Politics*; Schrijvers, *The Way of the Rat*; Greene, *The 48 Laws of Power*.

| Lesson | Outcome | Guided practice |
|---|---|---|
| 01 — Formal versus informal power | Map decision authority, expertise, resources and dependencies | Analyse a fictional organisation with conflicting reporting lines |
| 02 — Reputation and coalitions | Evaluate how trust, competence and stakeholder alignment affect influence | Compare two transparent approaches to building support for a proposal |
| 03 — Agenda and information asymmetry | Identify unequal information access and governance risks | Audit a fictional committee process for omitted perspectives and information gaps |
| 04 — Organisational political judgement | Compare legitimate advocacy with retaliation, favouritism or sabotage | Produce an accountable stakeholder engagement and escalation plan |

**Assessment:** stakeholder and governance map for a fictional budget decision. Criteria: correct power map; recognition of constraints; feasible coalition based on shared interests; accountability and fair process. Treat popular maxims as propositions to test rather than laws.

## SP-06 — Game theory, leverage and negotiation

**Canonical anchors:** [COM-106 negotiation](../curriculum/COM-106/syllabus.md), [INT-108 forecasting and decisions](../curriculum/INT-108/syllabus.md), [LEA-107 strategy and execution](../curriculum/LEA-107/syllabus.md). **Reading:** Dixit and Nalebuff, *The Art of Strategy*; Schelling, *The Strategy of Conflict*; Fisher, Ury and Patton, *Getting to Yes*.

| Lesson | Outcome | Guided practice |
|---|---|---|
| 01 — Strategic interdependence | Build and interpret a two-player payoff matrix from explicit assumptions | Compare cooperation and competition in a hypothetical supplier contract |
| 02 — Alternatives and bargaining power | Estimate best alternatives to agreement, reservation values and uncertainty | Calculate a fictional bargaining range and identify conditions where no deal is better |
| 03 — Commitment, credibility and repeated games | Distinguish enforceable commitments from unsupported assertions | Compare two contract proposals for credibility, incentives and long-term consequences |
| 04 — Negotiation under asymmetric information | Develop transparent discovery questions and objective agreement criteria | Conduct an opt-in mock negotiation with a right to walk away |

**Assessment:** negotiation dossier, payoff analysis and simulated negotiation. Criteria: internally consistent assumptions; viable alternatives; justified decision; transparent agreement and respect for refusal. Include sensitivity analysis rather than assuming all participants are rational or have known preferences.

## SP-07 — Chase Hughes: critical study of behavioural systems

**Canonical anchors:** INT-104, [INT-109 epistemology](../curriculum/INT-109/syllabus.md), COM-103, EMO-106. **Reading:** all four Hughes works listed below; Bond and DePaulo (2006); Vrij, Hartwig and Granhag (2019).

| Lesson | Outcome | Guided practice |
|---|---|---|
| 01 — Six-Minute X-Ray | Evaluate rapid-profiling claims against alternative explanations and observable evidence | Construct a claim-evidence-limitation table using an independently obtained edition |
| 02 — The Ellipsis Manual | Distinguish conceptual claims about influence, hypnosis and compliance from established empirical evidence | Identify claims requiring independent tests and examine implications for informed consent |
| 03 — Behavior Operations Manual | Compare proprietary behavioural models with established research standards | Audit definitions, falsifiability, sample selection, prediction accuracy and independent replication |
| 04 — Collected Works and synthesis | Evaluate consistency across the author's works and distinguish anecdote from evidence | Write a critical synthesis including strongest supporting evidence and unresolved questions |

**Assessment:** comparative evidence review covering all four texts. Criteria: precise edition and claim locators; accurate evidence classification; independent corroboration or explicit uncertainty; limitations and ethical scope. Reading author materials is not proof of efficacy. Do not practise covert hypnosis, interrogation or profiling of real individuals.

## SP-08 — Integrated strategic judgement

**Canonical anchors:** LEA-107, [LEA-109 governance](../curriculum/LEA-109/syllabus.md), [PHI-108 practical judgement](../curriculum/PHI-108/syllabus.md). **Reading:** revisit Pfeffer, Dixit and Nalebuff, Cialdini, Greene, Hughes and Simon.

| Lesson | Outcome | Guided practice |
|---|---|---|
| 01 — Strategic diagnosis | Integrate facts, incentives, power relationships and uncertainty in a situation model | Build a fact/hypothesis register and stakeholder map for a fictional enterprise |
| 02 — Options and consequences | Compare strategic options across short- and long-term effects | Evaluate a decision matrix including trust, fairness, reversibility and feasibility |
| 03 — Adversarial review | Stress-test a proposal against alternative explanations, counterarguments and exploitation risks | Act as reviewer of a fictional case; document failure modes and protective controls |
| 04 — Execution and adaptation | Define milestones, feedback, review and stop conditions for a transparent strategy | Draft an implementation plan and revise after new fictional evidence |

**Assessment:** integrated decision memo. Criteria: grounded diagnosis; multiple feasible options; explicit consequences and limitations; responsible adaptation.

## Integrative capstone — Strategic decision under pressure

**Scenario:** a fictional project team has competing budget priorities, an influential informal stakeholder, a supplier imposing a short decision window, a disputed claim about another person's intentions and incomplete commercial information. The learner must recommend a course of action, not obtain agreement at any cost.

Deliver a situation map, observation versus inference ledger, influence-risk audit, negotiation alternatives and payoff model, decision memo, safeguards and an after-action review. Include at least one counterexample, one change in assumptions and a delayed reassessment on a new scenario.

| Criterion | Observable Level 2 evidence | Level 3 extension |
|---|---|---|
| Evidence calibration | Separates facts and hypotheses with guidance | Revises independently under contradictory evidence |
| Power and incentives | Maps relevant stakeholders, authority and alternatives | Models strategic interdependence in an unfamiliar case |
| Influence and coercion | Recognises pressure, missing information and boundaries | Justifies a proportionate response in a new case |
| Negotiation | Presents truthful options, trade-offs and viable exit | Negotiates a novel simulated case without prompts |
| Strategic judgement | Selects and explains an accountable approach | Adapts approach under uncertainty and documents consequences |

Apply the existing [assessment policy](../framework/assessment-policy.md): all criteria at Level 2; no averaging away missing evidence or safety failures. Level 3 requires independent transfer, delayed reassessment and qualified human judgement. These elective criteria are proposed and do not award canonical competencies. No private disclosures, real targets, covert experiments, pressure on unwilling participants or claims of mind-reading ability. Assess strategic reasoning and informed decisions, not success at controlling another person.
